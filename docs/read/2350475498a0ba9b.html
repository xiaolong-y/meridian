Carahsoft Builds Federal Quantum Catalog with IonQ and PQSecure Partnerships

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 23h quantum 448 words 3 min read

Government IT solutions provider Carahsoft Technology Corp. has added trapped-ion hardware developer IonQ (NYSE: IONQ) and post-quantum security vendor PQSecure Technologies to its public sector distribution network. The parallel agreements establish standardized procurement routes for U.S. federal civilian agencies, defense departments, intelligence bodies, and state and local institutions seeking to deploy both active quantum hardware and quantum-resistant cryptographic defenses.
| Carahsoft Public Sector Quantum Portfolio: Compute & Cryptography Layers | | |
|---|---|---|
| Channel Dimension | IonQ Platform Integration | PQSecure Technologies Integration |
| Core Technology Scope | Trapped-ion quantum computing, optical networking hardware, sensing, and security protocols. | Post-quantum cryptographic (PQC) algorithms, synthesizable silicon IP cores, and software libraries. |
| Delivery Modalities | Cloud API access, hybrid HPC cluster integration, and dedicated quantum network nodes. | FPGA/ASIC/SoC IP, side-channel protected firmware, and C/Rust embedded cryptographic modules. |
| Federal Compliance & Validation | DARPA Quantum Benchmarking Initiative (Stage C) and AFRL Rome quantum network testbeds. | NIST Cryptographic Algorithm Validation Program (CAVP) and defense agency CIO program listings. |
| Contract Vehicles & Access | GSA Schedules, NASA SEWP, and enterprise government reseller channels. | TIPS (Contract #220105), OMNIA Partners (#R240303), and E&I Cooperative (#EI00063~2021MA). |
Direct federal procurement of quantum assets often stalls over non-standard licensing terms, classification hurdles, and complex agency bidding requirements. As master government aggregator, Carahsoft addresses these operational barriers by onboarding commercial quantum vendors onto pre-negotiated, multi-agency contract vehicles, allowing agency buyers and federal systems integrators (FSIs) to purchase hardware, IP, and support under uniform pricing and compliance terms.
The addition of IonQ provides agencies with a pathway to procure full-stack trapped-ion systems, spanning quantum processors such as the barium-based Superion system, field-deployable quantum sensors, and optical networking hardware currently evaluated by the Air Force Research Laboratory (AFRL) in Rome, New York. The partnership addresses operational mandates outlined in Executive Order 14413, Ushering in the Next Frontier of Quantum Innovation, which tasks civil and defense agencies with integrating domestic quantum computing and communications assets.
Concurrently, the PQSecure agreement addresses the defensive side of national quantum strategy: securing existing government infrastructure against future cryptanalytic threats. PQSecure’s catalog provides NIST CAVP-validated post-quantum cryptography spanning C, Rust, and assembly libraries alongside synthesizable IP cores for FPGA and ASIC platforms. Designed for defense electronics, aerospace systems, and long-lifecycle critical infrastructure, the offerings feature crypto-agile architectures, hardware Root-of-Trust security, and physical countermeasures against side-channel and fault-injection attacks.
Together, the dual appointments indicate that public sector quantum procurement is transitioning from ad-hoc exploratory research grants to standardized enterprise IT and cybersecurity frameworks.
