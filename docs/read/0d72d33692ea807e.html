Quantinuum and RIKEN Execute End-to-End Hybrid Quantum-HPC Chemistry on Fugaku and Reimei

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 10h quantum 559 words 3 min read

Quantinuum and RIKEN have demonstrated an integrated quantum-classical workflow linking Japan’s flagship supercomputer, Fugaku, with Quantinuum’s on-premises Reimei trapped-ion quantum processor. Detailed in an arXiv preprint titled “Quantum-HPC hybrid computation of biomolecular excited-state energies,” the collaboration executed a layered electronic-structure calculation simulating biomolecular excited states, integrating high-performance classical nodes with a physical quantum coprocessor via direct-access APIs.

Simulating complex chemical reactions—such as photoisomerization in visual reception and enzyme catalysis—presents a structural divide: the reactive active site exhibits strong multiconfigurational electron correlation requiring high-level quantum mechanical treatment, while the surrounding molecular scaffolding is large but weakly correlated. The project, commissioned under the New Energy and Industrial Technology Development Organization (NEDO) initiative, resolved this boundary by pairing classical supercomputing with quantum coprocessing inside the ONIOM (Our own N-layered Integrated molecular Orbital and Molecular mechanics) computational framework.
In this workflow, 96 Fugaku nodes performed initial molecular geometry optimizations and evaluated baseline environment energy contributions for a retinal molecule embedded in a carbon nanotube matrix. To compute the strongly correlated active site, the system deployed TE-QSCI on Reimei. Rather than relying on single-reference ansatz states, the algorithm initialized an approximate complete active space wavefunction on 16 logical qubits and evolved it under the target electronic Hamiltonian. This process sampled electronic configurations across higher-order Krylov subspaces that were subsequently returned to Fugaku for subspace Hamiltonian matrix diagonalization.
Logical circuits were protected using an [[18, 16, 2]] error-detection code, filtering weight-one faults through measurement post-selection. Out of 12,214 shots, TE-QSCI identified 2,149 new electronic configurations outside the initial classical active space, accurately capturing the leading six configurations of the full CASCI(8,8) reference. The resulting eigenstate fidelities reached 0.994 for the singlet ground state (S0), 0.995 for the first singlet excited state (S1), and 0.996 for the triplet ground state (T0), aligning excitation energies within 0.03 eV of exact benchmarks.
The implementation replaces cloud-based multi-tenant job queues with direct API orchestration, demonstrating coordinated job scheduling, error-detected execution, and hybrid data interchange across an active national supercomputing center.
