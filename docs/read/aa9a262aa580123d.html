Coherence Quantum Launches with €2.78 Million ($3.1 Million USD) Seed Round to Scale Cryogenic Quantum Control

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 22h quantum 291 words 2 min read

Finnish quantum hardware startup Coherence Quantum has officially launched out of stealth with €2.78 million (~$3.122 million USD) in seed financing to develop integrated cryogenic signaling and control infrastructure for solid-state quantum processors.
The funding round was led by Helsinki-based early-stage venture firm Lifeline Ventures, with participation from technology entrepreneur Mårten Mickos. Spun out of Aalto University’s Quantum Computing and Devices (QCD) research group in Espoo, Finland, the company focuses on overcoming the thermal, wiring, and density bottlenecks that constrain large-scale superconducting and spin-qubit quantum computers inside dilution refrigerators.
• Monolithic cryogenic control circuits near the QPU
• Minimizes line counts running from room temperature, reducing heat dissipation and control latency.
As solid-state quantum processors scale from hundreds to thousands of physical qubits, control lines running from room-temperature electronics into dilution fridges create severe space, thermal load, and signal distortion constraints. Coherence Quantum is building modular, low-noise signal-conditioning components and high-density interfaces designed to sit close to the quantum processing unit (QPU), aiming to establish a standardized blueprint for scalable cryogenic control architectures.
The company will use the capital to establish its dedicated laboratory, testing, and manufacturing facilities in Espoo, accelerate prototype fabrication, and expand its engineering team.
