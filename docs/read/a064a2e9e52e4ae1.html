Maryland Budget Secures Millions to Consolidate “Capital of Quantum” Status

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 2d quantum 344 words 2 min read

Governor Wes Moore has signed Maryland’s Fiscal Year 2027 state budget, directing tens of millions of dollars toward the state’s Capital of Quantum initiative. The enacted budget aims to transition the regional economy away from its traditional reliance on “eds, feds, and meds” by doubling down on quantum infrastructure and commercialization. Since the initiative’s launch in January 2025—a strategic partnership targeting over $1 billion in total state, federal, and private investments—Maryland has successfully secured more than $500 million in sector-specific funding, including a high-profile agreement with Microsoft and a matching grant partnership with DARPA.
The FY 2027 budget includes several direct investments to expand the state’s quantum footprint in College Park and Prince George’s County:
- IonQ Expansion: $20 million allocated as part of a multi-year $50 million commitment to construct IonQ’s new global headquarters in College Park.
- University of Maryland (UMD) Infrastructure: $22 million for the UMD Enterprise Corporation to expand the Quantum Start-Up Foundry and establish national quantum testbeds, alongside $20 million for a dedicated Deep Tech Facility in the Discovery District.
- ARLIS and Faculty Recruitment: $12 million to support the Applied Research Laboratory for Intelligence and Security (ARLIS) and recruit specialist quantum faculty to the University of Maryland. This follows the recent groundbreaking of a $65 million, 110,000-square-foot ARLIS headquarters focused on AI and quantum applications for national defense.
The budget also supports broader ecosystem milestones, such as the establishment of IQM Quantum Computers’ first U.S. technology center in the UMD Discovery District and a $7.5 million expansion of the IonQ-UMD QLab collaboration to include advanced quantum networking. By integrating state-funded academic leadership with commercial-grade systems, Maryland is positioning itself as the primary domestic testbed for quantum hardware and software, with the goal of creating thousands of high-skill jobs and resolving global security challenges through the application of quantum science.
