EPFL Demonstrates Millimeter-Wave-to-Optical Transducer with 67% Internal Efficiency

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 9h quantum 569 words 3 min read

Researchers at the École Polytechnique Fédérale de Lausanne (EPFL) Hybrid Photonics Laboratory and Center for Quantum Science and Engineering have demonstrated an integrated electro-optic transducer bridging the millimeter-wave (mm-wave) and telecom optical domains. Detailed in the paper “Coherent transduction and dynamical back-action between light and millimeter waves,” the architecture couples a thin-film lithium niobate (TFLN) photonic molecule to a sub-millimeter metallic resonator, achieving a vacuum electro-optic coupling rate of approximately 60 kHz and an internal conversion efficiency of 67%.

Coherent frequency conversion between high-frequency electric fields and optical carriers serves as a critical interface for distributed quantum architectures, linking cryogenic processors and neutral-atom registers over optical fiber without introducing thermal load. While vacuum electro-optic coupling scales with operating frequency according to g0 ∝ (ωmmw / Vmmw)1/2, conventional implementations suffered from mode-volume mismatch: sub-millimeter electromagnetic fields were paired with millimeter-scale optical racetrack resonators, degrading field overlap and reducing net cooperativity.
The EPFL design resolves this spatial discrepancy by defining two compact Fabry–Pérot microcavities via photonic crystal mirrors etched into a suspended 400 nm TFLN membrane. Integrated DC tuning electrodes adjust the residual detuning between the resulting symmetric and anti-symmetric modes to align the doublet splitting with the adjacent quarter-wavelength resonator. Complete polarization alignment along the crystalline Z-axis maximizes interaction with lithium niobate’s primary Pockels tensor component (r33).
Because the optical linewidth is roughly two orders of magnitude narrower than the dissipative mm-wave resonance (γmmw / 2π ≈ 15 GHz), the transducer operates in the reversed dissipation regime (κ± ≪ γmmw). The optical filter isolates the narrow spectral slice of room-temperature thermal mm-wave photons falling within its bandwidth, enabling the thermal sideband to function as an uncalibrated internal standard for measuring device cooperativity.
Under coherent drive, electro-optic dynamical back-action modulates the optical response: the beam-splitter up-conversion interaction introduces an additional dissipation path that broadens the optical linewidth, while parametric down-conversion anti-damps and narrows the mode. Although total off-chip wireless efficiency remains constrained by free-space beam coupling into the integrated antenna (1.6 × 10−4), transitioning from a normal-metal gold resonator to high-gap superconductors such as niobium titanium nitride (NbTiN) is projected to suppress internal ohmic damping and increase total transduction efficiency at liquid-helium temperatures.
