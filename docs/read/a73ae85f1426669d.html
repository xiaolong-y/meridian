D-Wave Reports Its Q2 2026 Financial Results Showing Bookings Surge, Technical Progress, and Quantum Circuits Acquisition Impact

Quantum Computing Report dougfinke 13h quantum 562 words 3 min read

D-Wave Quantum Inc. reported its financial results for the second quarter and first half of 2026, highlighting significant commercial momentum and rapid growth in long-term commitments. While quarterly revenue remained flat due to system sale timing, the company saw dramatic expansion in its backlog, enterprise engagements, and technical roadmaps across both annealing and gate-model quantum architectures.

Financial Performance & Commercial Expansion
The primary highlight of the report is D-Wave’s massive increase in bookings and backlog. First-half bookings surged over 1,120% year-over-year to $35.5 million, anchored by a new $20 million system sale and $2.3 million contributed from the acquisition of Quantum Circuits, Inc. As a result, Remaining Performance Obligations (RPOs) jumped 668% to $40.7 million, with 57% expected to convert to recognized revenue over the next 12 months.
Commercial adoption continues to shift from experimental pilots to production workloads. Commercial customers generated 67.7% of first-half revenue (up from 16.0% in H1 2025), with Forbes Global 2000 clients accounting for nearly half of total revenue. Production applications now represent 37.3% of total Quantum Computing as a Service (QCaaS) revenue. Net loss narrowed significantly to $48.0 million for the quarter, largely due to reduced non-cash warrant liability charges, while operating expenses grew to support aggressive R&D and go-to-market scaling. D-Wave closed the period with $546.2 million in cash and investments, after utilizing capital for the Quantum Circuits transaction.
Dual-Platform Hardware & Software Roadmaps
On the technical front, D-Wave expanded plans for both its annealing and gate-model architectures. For its Advantage3™ annealing track, the company outlined a multi-chip fabric design utilizing superconducting interconnects to scale beyond single-chip limits, targeting 20,000 qubits by 2029 and 100,000 qubits by 2031.
In gate-model quantum computing, D-Wave published peer-reviewed research in Nature detailing a high-fidelity two-qubit gate built on its dual-rail superconducting architecture, which reduces hardware overhead for fault tolerance. The company set explicit milestones for gate-model deployment, targeting 17 physical qubits in 2026, 181 physical qubits by 2028, 10 logical qubits by 2030, and a 100-logical-qubit system capable of over one million operations by 2032. Complementing the hardware, D-Wave announced an upcoming error-aware simulator to assist developers in building error-resilient algorithms.
The quarter also brought additional government funding and enterprise customer traction including a $1.57 million NSF grant under the NQVL ERASE project, second-year defense funding via NORDTECH, and key enterprise customer wins with organizations including AT&T, Nasdaq Verafin, Unisys, Shionogi, Oki Electric, and a major entertainment brand.
