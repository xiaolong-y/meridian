South Korea Launches 614 PFLOPS Hangang Supercomputer Ahead of May 2027 IonQ Quantum Link

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 8h quantum 545 words 3 min read

South Korea’s Ministry of Science and ICT (MSIT) and the Korea Institute of Science and Technology Information (KISTI) have officially inaugurated the country’s sixth national supercomputer, named Hangang (漢江). Installed at KISTI headquarters in Daejeon, the direct-liquid-cooled HPE Cray EX400 system achieves a theoretical peak performance of 614 petaflops—a 24-fold increase over its predecessor, Nurion. Crucially, Hangang is designed from inception to operate as the high-performance computing (HPC) backbone of South Korea’s first hybrid quantum-classical computing platform, scheduled to interface on-site with IonQ’s Tempo trapped-ion quantum processor by May 2027.

Hangang’s core architecture leverages Nvidia’s GH200 superchips, combining an Arm-based Grace CPU and a Hopper GPU on a single coherent package via a 900 GB/s bidirectional interconnect. This architecture eliminates PCIe memory transfer bottlenecks by exposing a unified 576 GB address space to the operating system, allowing large-scale molecular simulations and materials calculations to reside entirely within chip memory. During early pilot benchmarks, research teams achieved up to a 41.8× performance speedup over prior-generation A100 GPU clusters on memory-bound workloads.
The system’s quantum interface realizes the trilateral agreement executed between KISTI, Nvidia, and IonQ in March 2026. Under the deployment roadmap, Hangang will connect to an on-site IonQ Tempo system using Nvidia’s NVQLink and the CUDA-Q programming environment. Tempo’s trapped barium-ion architecture provides all-to-all qubit connectivity, eliminating routing SWAP gate overheads when mapping non-local Hamiltonian terms. In practice, the hybrid environment will delegate classical data preparation, parameter updates, and post-processing to Hangang’s GH200 nodes while offloading structured quantum subroutines—such as molecular electronic structure ground states and combinatorial optimization phases—directly to Tempo.
Hangang anchors the South Korean government’s nationwide K-Moonshot initiative, a program supported by ₩10.1 trillion (~$7.5 billion USD) in 2026 AI R&D funding aimed at accelerating discoveries across 12 national challenges, including battery materials discovery, clean energy, and quantum computing. Following pilot trials serving approximately 190 projects, Hangang will open for full production access on December 14, 2026, dedicating 15% of its compute footprint to small and medium enterprises (SMEs) and commercial startups.
