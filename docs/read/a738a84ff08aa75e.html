Yonsei University Researchers Develop Mamba State-Space Neural Decoder to Enable Real-Time Quantum Error Correction

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 1h quantum 449 words 3 min read

Researchers from Yonsei University have developed a neural quantum error correction (QEC) decoder based on the Mamba structured state-space architecture, reducing the computational complexity of deep-learning decoding from O(d4) to O(d2) in code distance d. In peer-reviewed research published in npj Quantum Information, the team demonstrated that the state-space model matches the accuracy of Transformer-based models such as AlphaQubit on experimental memory benchmarks while eliminating the quadratic self-attention bottlenecks that prevent real-time feedback on physical quantum hardware.
State-Space Syndrome Processing and Real-Time Decoding Metrics
In planar surface codes, syndrome extraction generates sequences scaling with the number of stabilizers (Nstab = d2 − 1). Standard Transformer neural decoders utilize multi-head self-attention mechanisms that perform pairwise comparisons between all stabilizer tokens, yielding an O(d4) scaling that accumulates latency backlogs well beyond microsecond superconducting coherence limits. The Mamba decoder replaces explicit multi-head attention with selective state-space layers, utilizing input-dependent linear scans to maintain historical syndrome contexts within compressed hidden states. Evaluated on the Google Quantum AI Sycamore hardware dataset, the state-space architecture matched Transformer accuracy in static memory benchmarks and maintained an error-suppression advantage when accounting for decoder-induced latency noise.

Inference Latency Limits and Hardware Deployment Constraints
The performance divergence under simulated real-time conditions underscores a foundational constraint in fault-tolerant quantum computing: when decoders cannot process syndromes within the physical cycle time (typically 200 to 1,000 ns on superconducting hardware), idling physical data qubits accumulate uncorrected environmental noise. Under the circuit-level depolarizing SI1000 model, the latency-induced error gap between the two neural models expanded monotonically from d = 3 through d = 9. However, while Mamba resolves the attention-scaling bottleneck, translating neural state-space decoders into physical control stacks requires overcoming edge-compute limitations. Practical implementation requires porting the recurrent core, dilated convolutions, and ResNet feature embedders onto low-latency field-programmable gate arrays (FPGAs) or custom application-specific integrated circuits (ASICs) to execute inference within sub-microsecond cycle budgets.
The peer-reviewed paper is available in npj Quantum Information, with open-source numerical datasets hosted on GitHub.
