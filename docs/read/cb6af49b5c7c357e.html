Aqarios Launches Constraint-Native Quantum Optimizer in the IBM Qiskit Functions Catalog

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 20h quantum 408 words 3 min read

Aqarios GmbH, a Munich-based quantum software spin-off of LMU Munich, has released its Constrained Quantum Optimizer in the IBM Qiskit Functions Catalog, giving IBM Quantum Premium, Flex, and on-premises users a managed solver for discrete combinatorial optimization. Built on the company’s FlexQAOA engine and validated in New Journal of Physics, the function ingests standard LP, MPS, or Luna Model files and enforces hard one-hot and set-packing constraints natively at the circuit level rather than relying solely on quadratic penalty terms.

Conventional quantum optimization workflows translate constrained problems into unconstrained binary models by adding penalty weights that expand the effective search space and require manual tuning. Aqarios’ solver instead implements Iterative Warm-Start XY-Mixer QAOA (IWS-QAOA), which aligns a biased initial state with a warm-started XY-mixer Hamiltonian to confine state evolution to the feasible Hamming-weight-1 subspace while using fixed linear-ramp schedules to eliminate variational parameter training loops on quantum hardware.
Prior to circuit synthesis, an automated preprocessor performs graph clique merging and pendant-node variable fixing to compress qubit counts, converts remaining constraints using structure-derived penalty weights, and applies greedy steepest-descent post-processing to repair noise-induced constraint violations. In Qiskit Functions Catalog evaluations on IBM Heron hardware, replacing variational training with iterative warm-starting reduced QPU compute times from 130 to 200 seconds down to roughly 30 seconds on medium and large problem tiers.
