Forschungszentrum Jülich Demonstrates 6.8-Nanosecond Single-Qubit Gates with > 99.98% Fidelity via Multi-Photon Leakage Suppression

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 9h quantum 475 words 3 min read

Physicists at Forschungszentrum Jülich, RWTH Aachen University, and the University of Cologne have achieved single-qubit gate operations lasting 6.8 nanoseconds with gate fidelities exceeding 99.98% on a superconducting transmon processor. Detailed in Nature Communications under the title Ultrafast single-qubit gates through multi-photon transition removal, the study introduces the “R2D” pulse-shaping method to suppress multi-photon leakage into non-computational states (|2⟩ and |3⟩) in the strongly driven regime. Conducted within the German national QSOLID project, the work sets a speed record for high-fidelity transmon operations by cutting unwanted leakage transitions by more than a factor of 20 compared to standard DRAG protocols.

In weakly anharmonic superconducting transmons (operating here at Δ / 2π = −225 MHz), driving gates faster requires increasing microwave power. In this strongly driven limit, the drive field shifts the qubit’s dressed eigenstates, causing direct two-photon transitions (|0⟩ → |2⟩ and |1⟩ → |3⟩) that bypass traditional selection rules and degrade conventional Derivative Removal by Adiabatic Gate (DRAG) corrections. The Jülich team’s R2D method introduces a double recursive derivation that nulls spectral components at multi-photon transition frequencies using three in-situ optimized parameters (α12, α02, α13). Paired with an Amplified Leakage Error (ALE) sequence that isolates coherent leakage from thermal excitations, the protocol pushes gate speed while keeping coherent control errors well below the environmental decoherence floor (T1 = 40 μs, T2Echo = 56 μs).
While the 6.8-nanosecond gate addresses single-qubit limits on isolated transmons, scaling the technique across multi-qubit lattices introduces practical compiler hurdles. Operating at normalized drive strengths of Ω / |Δ| = 0.33 risks spectator-qubit dephasing, microwave cross-talk, and frequency crowding across densely packed planar chips. Adapting R2D to multi-qubit systems will require integrating multi-frequency spectral notch filters into automated calibration pipelines and evaluating whether similar recursive pulse corrections can mitigate leakage during nanosecond-scale two-qubit entangling gates.
