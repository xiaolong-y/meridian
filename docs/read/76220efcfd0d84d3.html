Unitary Foundation Releases 2026 Quantum Open Source Survey Covering SDK Adoption, Hybrid HPC Workflows, and AI Tooling

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 13h quantum 660 words 4 min read

Unitary Foundation has published the results of its fifth annual 2026 Quantum Open Source Survey, synthesizing responses from more than 1,800 developers, researchers, and enterprise practitioners worldwide collected between September 10 and October 29, 2026. Developed by Verdiana Pasqualini and Otho Mantegazza and released under an Open Database License, the dataset tracks multi-year shifts in software adoption while introducing new benchmarks on hybrid quantum-classical pipelines, quantum error correction (QEC), resource estimation, and generative AI usage.

Beyond individual software package rankings, the 2026 dataset highlights several structural shifts across the global quantum developer base:
- Workforce Composition & Languages: Researchers (49.4%) and software developers (34.2%) form the largest role cohorts, with 46.6% working in quantum technology as a primary paid job and 56.2% reporting two or fewer years of professional quantum experience. Geographically, the United States (17.7%), India (15.7%), Israel (15.5%), Canada (7.7%), the United Kingdom (5.1%), and Germany (5.0%) lead participation. Python dominates active development at 92.5%, followed by C/C++ (up from 27.8% in 2025 to 33.9% in 2026) and Rust (14.0% active, and ranked first at 45.3% among languages respondents plan to learn).
- Hybrid Quantum-Classical & HPC Execution: Over half of respondents (52.9%) ran hybrid workflows during the past year, spanning CPU simulators (80.2%), GPU-accelerated simulators (56.4%), QPU execution with classical pre- and post-processing (46.8%), iterative loops (36.7%), and real-time feedback pipelines (21.3%). Among the 36.8% utilizing high-performance computing (HPC) tools, NVIDIA CUDA (24.1%) and Slurm (17.0%) are the most common frameworks, though limited QPU access (54.4%), compute cost (41.8%), constrained GPU/HPC availability (37.0%), and quantum-classical integration friction (25.4%) remain top scaling bottlenecks.
- AI Verification & Research Priorities: While practitioners rated AI productivity gains at 3.1 out of 4.0, confidence in code accuracy (2.5) and conceptual explanations (2.6) remains cautious. As a result, 42.9% always verify AI-generated quantum outputs and 38.7% verify most of the time, relying on manual review (78.3%) and automated tests (54.2%) rather than formal verification (17.5%). Looking forward, respondents identified algorithm development (54.3%), quantum error correction (44.4%), and application development (42.6%) as top research priorities alongside calls for open-source hardware characterization, domain libraries, and standardized benchmarks.
