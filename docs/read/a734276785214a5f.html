QuEra Computing and HPE Partner to Integrate Fault-Tolerant Neutral-Atom QPUs with On-Premises HPE Cray Supercomputers

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 5h quantum 312 words 2 min read

Neutral-atom quantum hardware developer QuEra Computing and enterprise infrastructure provider Hewlett Packard Enterprise (HPE) have entered into a strategic collaboration to integrate fault-tolerant quantum processing units (QPUs) with on-premises high-performance computing (HPC) environments powered by the HPE Cray supercomputing platform. The partnership establishes an engineering framework for national laboratories, HPC centers, and enterprise data centers to deploy tightly coupled, hybrid quantum-classical supercomputers.
The joint technical effort focuses on developing integrated hardware testbeds, co-designing hybrid algorithms, establishing low-latency software interoperability, and benchmarking system-level performance when pairing QuEra’s neutral-atom architecture directly with HPE Cray system interconnects. Deploying fault-tolerant QPUs on-premises addresses strict sovereign data residency and latency requirements, allowing high-density classical HPC clusters to offload specific compute-bound subproblems to neutral-atom hardware. The initiative aligns with U.S. national scientific programs, including the U.S. Department of Energy’s Genesis Mission.

Under this strategy, organizations can utilize cloud-based access to QuEra’s upcoming Libra system—a megaquop-class, 256+ logical qubit processor targeted for 2028 availability—to develop algorithms and application expertise, while transitioning production workloads to on-premises HPE Cray clusters as physical hardware is installed.
