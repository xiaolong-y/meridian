Every call
Caller: Hello, am I speaking with Dr. Aaron … [reading from notes] … I mean, uhh … Scott … Aaronson?
Me: Yes.
Caller: Oh, hi Dr. Aaronson! I hope you were having a nice Thanksgiving dinner before I interrupted it. This is Rich Midwit calling from Snake-in-the-Grass Financial Newsletter. On behalf of my readers at SITG, I was wondering whether I might pick your brain for a few minutes about…
Me: Is this about whether the AI labs broke RSA?
Rich: … what I was about to say, Dr. Aaronson, was actually just that I was curious to understand … err, yeah. It’s about RSA. Are the AI labs sitting on a break, or aren’t they?
Me: Look, I have specific information that the labs will soon release a major breakthrough in theoretical cryptanalysis—which breakthrough, however, will not lead to imminent danger for any currently deployed cryptosystems. I wish I could tell you more about that, but I can’t on the record. And then, separately from that, I’ve heard endless rumors and speculations about what else the labs might have, including breaks that would cause imminent danger if they were real. And I’m forced to take such rumors seriously, given how many previous rumors about mathematical breakthroughs at the labs have turned out to be correct, and given also that, just on priors, it would be astonishing good luck if all our current crypto protocols managed to survive the onslaught of the newly superhuman AI mathematicians without a scratch. But I don’t have any specific, actionable information there. See, I’m trying to be both truthful and responsible, which is hard because…
Rich: So you’re telling me that the proof of VP=VPN doesn’t break cryptography?
Me: Oh God. A VPN is a Virtual Private Network. VNP, by contrast, is Valiant’s algebraic version of NP, and it was spectacularly proved earlier this week to not equal VP, which is Valiant’s algebraic version of P. So, this is a step toward proving P ≠ NP, which would ironically open up the first-ever possibility of proving modern cryptography secure!
Rich: So you’re telling me that, if AI proves P ≠ NP, we’ll know that RSA and all the other encryption codes are secure?
Me: No, that’s not what I said. P ≠ NP is necessary for secure cryptography based on hard problems, but it’s not sufficient. See, there was this guy Impagliazzo who in 1995 laid out five possible worlds. The first world, Cryptomania, is the one where…
Rich: … I’m sorry to cut you off, Dr. Aaronson, but this is getting way too complicated. Let me rephrase my question as, which cryptocurrencies should people buy right now, and which ones should they dump?
Me: Begone.
Rich: Excuse me?
Me: I mean, give me your info, and I will Venmo you $0.25, since you must make immediate gain from anything you learn.
Rich: Oh, no no no, I didn’t mean it that way! I just meant that I was … uhh … intellectually curious to understand the, like, theoretical basis of these various blockchains. I mean, it’s fascinating that AI agents might soon break some blockchains but not others, isn’t it? So if I could just get you to speculate for a bit about…
Me: Sorry, gotta run now. I see my mom brought out the stuffing and jello mold, and I’m intellectually curious to know how they taste, so another time!
