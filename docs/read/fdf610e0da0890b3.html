Universal Quantum Unveils the iQPU: Foundry-Fabricated Trapped-Ion Processor Engineered for Modular Scaling

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 1d quantum 319 words 2 min read

Trapped-ion hardware developer Universal Quantum has commercially launched the iQPU (Integrated Quantum Processing Unit), a monolithic processor architecture designed to eliminate the physical wiring bottleneck in large-scale trapped-ion systems. The chips will serve as the core compute engine for two quantum computers currently being delivered under a €67 million ($73 million) contract with the German Aerospace Center (DLR).
Coherently links multiple discrete iQPU modules to operate as a unified, multi-chip quantum processor.
Customer Deployment
DLR System Delivery
Supports institutional projects across space mission planning, climate modeling, and materials design.
Trapped-ion quantum computing achieves high operational fidelities through natural atomic homogeneity and long coherence times, but scaling beyond dozens of qubits typically induces severe optical and electronic congestion. Delivering independent radio-frequency, direct-current, and laser channels from external cryostat ports creates a physical “wiring wall.” Universal Quantum addresses this by combining its Quantum Charge-Coupled Device (QCCD) layout with integrated control silicon, dynamically shuttling ions across the substrate to execute gates without dedicated beam paths for every qubit site.
To scale beyond the thermal and physical dimensions of a single chip, the architecture uses the company’s UQConnect interface to link adjacent modules without degrading global register coherence. Following the company’s recent $100 million Series A round, the launch positions Universal Quantum alongside Quantinuum and IonQ in the transition from bespoke laboratory assemblies to standardized, foundry-compatible multi-core QCCD engines.
