Alice & Bob Reports Phase 1 Results of France’s PROQCIMA Quantum Program

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 6h quantum 419 words 3 min read

Fault-tolerant quantum computing developer Alice & Bob has published a summary of technical milestones from Phase 1 of PROQCIMA, the French government’s 10-year, €500 million ($561 million USD) quantum procurement framework managed by the Defense Innovation Agency (AID) and the Direction générale de l’Armement (DGA).
Following a May 2026 national quantum strategy update, the French government officially accelerated PROQCIMA’s target roadmap from 128 logical qubits to 1,000 logical qubits by 2032, doubling the program’s annual budget. Stage 1 (2024–2028) allocated €60 million ($67.3 million USD) across the Phase 1 “examination” period for the five competing French hardware candidates (Alice & Bob, Pasqal, Quandela, Quobly, and C12), and is transitioning into Phase 2 (2026–2028)—a €150 million ($168.3 million USD) competitive phase (€30 million ($33.66 million USD) per company) that will narrow the cohort to three selected finalists before choosing the top two industrialization winners after 2030 (targeting 2,048 logical qubits).

To satisfy Phase 1 feasibility requirements, Alice & Bob operated three independent hardware demonstrators—two 6-qubit superconducting chips and one 12-qubit chip—evaluating the building blocks of its bosonic cat-qubit error-correction architecture. The company also constructed a digital twin of its processor using experimental telemetry data to establish the coherence and fidelity parameters required to operate below the fault-tolerant error threshold.
