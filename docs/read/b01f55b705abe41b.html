PsiQuantum and AMD Expand Partnership with Equity Investment to Co-Design Utility-Scale Quantum Infrastructure

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 12h quantum 400 words 3 min read

Photonic quantum computing developer PsiQuantum and semiconductor leader AMD (NASDAQ: AMD) have announced an expanded strategic partnership, accompanied by a planned equity investment from AMD, to co-design the classical and quantum computing infrastructure required for fault-tolerant quantum systems. Building on PsiQuantum’s existing adoption of AMD Versal adaptive system-on-chips, the collaboration integrates AMD Instinct GPUs, EPYC CPUs, and the ROCm open software stack with PsiQuantum’s silicon photonics architecture.
| PsiQuantum and AMD Hybrid Infrastructure & Co-Design Scope | | |
|---|---|---|
| Collaboration Layer | Technology / Platform | Operational & Strategic Function |
| Real-Time Error Correction | PRISM ML Decoder on AMD Versal Adaptive SoCs | Executes low-latency syndrome decoding on programmable hardware accelerators for utility-scale photonic QPUs. |
| Classical Compute Fabric | AMD Instinct GPUs, EPYC CPUs & ROCm Stack | Provides high-performance classical acceleration and unified software orchestration for hybrid workloads. |
| Target Application Domain | Covalent Inhibitor & Biological Modeling | Applies proprietary computational chemistry workflows on GPUs to oncology drug discovery under DOE Quantum Genesis. |
| Capital & Facility Footprint | AMD Equity Stake & Chicago IQMP Expansion | Reinforces commercial alignment and explores joint deployment at the Illinois Quantum and Microelectronics Park. |
Operating fault-tolerant photonic quantum computers at utility scale requires massive classical co-processing capacity to process error syndromes within strict optical clock cycles. Working alongside AMD engineers, PsiQuantum researchers tailored their machine-learning decoder, PRISM, to bridge classical logic and photonic control loops without introducing latency bottlenecks.
On the commercial application side, the companies are prioritizing pharmaceutical research and molecular simulation. By running PsiQuantum’s proprietary computational chemistry methods on high-performance GPUs, the partnership aims to deliver immediate classical simulation gains while preparing enterprise drug discovery pipelines for future fault-tolerant quantum hardware.
As part of their broader regional expansion, both organizations are evaluating a co-located presence in Chicago, where PsiQuantum serves as the anchor tenant for the state’s flagship quantum campus. The two companies will present further technical details on their joint roadmap at the SC26 supercomputing conference in Chicago from November 15 to 20.
