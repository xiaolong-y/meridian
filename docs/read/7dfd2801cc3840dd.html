UCLA Researchers Model Collectively Enhanced Superconducting-Qubit Interfaces for Microwave-to-Optical Transduction

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 13h quantum 309 words 2 min read

Researchers at the University of California, Los Angeles (UCLA) have published a theoretical and numerical study evaluating how ensembles of superconducting transmon qubits coupled collectively to a shared optomechanical resonator can enhance microwave-to-optical quantum transduction. Detailed in a technical preprint, the architecture addresses weak electromechanical coupling bottlenecks in optical interconnects designed to link cryogenic superconducting processors over low-loss fiber networks.
Rather than routing signals through a single intermediate microwave resonator, the proposed interface couples multiple electrically distinct superconducting qubits directly to a common mechanical normal mode via displacement-dependent electrodes, while an optical cavity reads out the mechanical vibration through radiation pressure. In weak-excitation input-output simulations across ensembles of 4, 24, and 64 qubits, collective coupling increased peak external conversion efficiency from approximately 20% to 30% and 40% while broadening conversion bandwidth, though optical pump-induced mechanical heating bounded the optimal pump strength.
Operating near half excitation in the symmetric Dicke manifold accelerates electromechanical transfer nearly linearly with ensemble size, but the authors showed that this speedup is offset by the proportional relaxation exposure of additional excited qubits, leaving single-qubit coupling and lifetime as the governing coherence requirement. To isolate single-excitation transitions, the team introduced an auxiliary transmon that induces mechanical Kerr anharmonicity, raising lossless single-photon optical target probability in a four-qubit benchmark from 19.6% to 99.7% at 100 kHz anharmonicity, while quantifying how pair-dependent qubit-qubit disorder reduces gated optical retrieval efficiency from 58.1% to 35.4%.
