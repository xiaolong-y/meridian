European Commission Proposes EU Quantum Act and Sovereign Tech Equity Fund to Scale Industrial Capacity by 2030

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 1d quantum 658 words 4 min read

The European Commission has formally proposed the EU Quantum Act (COM(2026) 965), the European Union’s first legislative framework dedicated to quantum technologies, alongside plans to establish a Sovereign Tech Equity Fund (STEF) targeted at up to €50 billion ($54.85 billion USD) across quantum, artificial intelligence, semiconductors, and cloud infrastructure. Presented by Executive Vice-President Henna Virkkunen, the regulation creates the Quantum Europe Framework Initiative to align national and Union programs, close Europe’s private growth-capital gap, and establish global industrial leadership by 2030.
| EU Quantum Act (COM(2026) 965) Legislative & Technical Architecture | | |
|---|---|---|
| Pillar / Instrument | Core Mechanism & Governance | Key Milestones, Budgets & Requirements |
| Financing & Sovereign Tech Equity Fund | Public-private equity vehicle with the EIB Group and Member States under the 2028–2034 MFF and European Competitiveness Fund. | Targets up to €50 billion across critical sovereign tech; Impact Assessment identifies €12.9B–€18.1B in European quantum financing needs (2028–2034). |
| Quantum Grand Challenges & R&I | Quantum Excellence Network and stage-gated Grand Challenges implemented via the EuroHPC Joint Undertaking (chips via Chips JU). | Targets ~100 error-corrected logical qubits per system by 2030 (scaling to thousands by 2035), GNSS-denied PNT by 2030, and 50 km metropolitan quantum networks. |
| Infrastructures & Industrial Scale-Up | European Quantum Service Platform, Strategic Projects, Quantum Innovation Hubs, and national Competence & Acceleration Centres. | Federates EuroHPC quantum systems, EuroQCI terrestrial-space QKD networks (by 2031), and Q-MRI clinical pilots; grants Strategic Projects the Competitiveness Seal. |
| Procurement, PQC & Supply Chains | Union-controlled procurement rules, NIS2 post-quantum cryptography mandates, and critical-component monitoring. | Requires PQC and crypto-agility in NIS2 public procurements six months after entry into force; authorizes a digital twin of the EU quantum supply chain. |
While the European Union hosts roughly one-third of the world’s quantum companies and committed an estimated €8.6 billion in public quantum funding through 2025, Commission figures show European startups attracted only €1.1 billion ($1.2 billion USD) (roughly 5% of global private investment) in 2025 compared with €7 billion ($7.67 billion USD) in the United States. To convert scientific output into domestic production, the proposal authorizes direct equity investments through the Sovereign Tech Equity Fund, designates cross-border Strategic Projects, and establishes a federated European Quantum Service Platform alongside specialized innovation hubs and national competence centers.
On security and industrial resilience, the regulation restricts public quantum procurement to Union-controlled suppliers (including system integrators) and reciprocal partners, empowers the Commission to mandate EU-sourced critical components, and requires NIS2-covered public administrations to procure post-quantum cryptographic and crypto-agile solutions starting six months after the law takes effect. The proposal also enables a supply-chain digital twin to track component chokepoints and grants the Commission delegated authority to impose binding post-quantum migration milestones if national transitions lag.
The proposal now moves to the European Parliament and the Council under the ordinary legislative procedure—with discussions continuing next week at the European Quantum Technologies Conference (EQTC) in Dublin during Ireland’s EU Council Presidency—while funding allocations will be settled during the 2028–2034 Multiannual Financial Framework negotiations. Welcoming the framework’s logical-qubit milestones and procurement rules, IQM Quantum Computers, Dublin-based silicon spin-qubit developer Equal1, and the European Quantum Industry Consortium (QuIC)—which represents over 240 member organizations and opens a two-week member consultation on November 27—urged lawmakers to ensure CMOS-manufactured quantum chips are fully supported alongside Chips Act 2.0, establish a dedicated quantum window in the Sovereign Tech Equity Fund, grant State aid eligibility for quantum chip foundries, and secure €7.7 billion to €12.3 billion in five-year public purchase commitments rewarding European design, manufacturing, and proven deployments.
