Zapata Quantum and University of Maryland Launch ForShor.ai Formal Verification Challenge for AI-Assisted Quantum Algorithm Design

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 23h quantum 432 words 3 min read

Zapata Quantum (OTCQB: ZPTA) and the University of Maryland (Joint Center for Quantum Information and Computer Science) have launched ForShor.ai, an open research challenge combining formal theorem proving with artificial intelligence to optimize quantum factoring subroutines. Developed with early grant support from the Unitary Foundation and scientific advisor Peter Shor, the initiative invites human researchers and AI agents to submit recursive arithmetic schedules to a public leaderboard, where an automated Lean 4 harness verifies mathematical correctness and asymptotic gate complexity before exporting circuits to Qualtran for logical resource estimation.
| ForShor Formal Verification Harness & Resource Estimation Specifications | | |
|---|---|---|
| Component / Benchmark | Technical Architecture & Scope | Verified Bounds & Resource Metrics |
| Lean 4 Verification Harness | ~180 Lean 4 files (~3,500 declarations, Apache 2.0) formalizing recursive Toom–Cook PhaseProduct and Cooley–Tukey QFT. | Proves end-to-end order-finding correctness and O(n2+ε) elementary gate scaling for any policy passing four decidable checks. |
| Policy Search Bottleneck | Exact exhaustive search over interpolation tables vs. heuristic/agentic policy optimization for n = 2048 bits. | Exact search at arity k = 8 requires ≥7.5 × 103 GiB memory and ~3.2 × 1051 core-hours, motivating AI-guided search. |
| Qualtran Logical Resource Estimates | Verified intermediate representation lowered to Qualtran Bloqs with Ross–Selinger T-gate synthesis (δ = 0.01). | 1.685 × 1012 T-gates at n = 2048 using varying arity k = (6, 5, 3), crossing Gidney–Ekerå discrete-log factoring at ~3.8 × 106 bits. |
| Open Challenge Scope | Six-week initial community challenge covering PhaseProduct (Shor) and PhaseTripleProduct (Regev). | Serves as a proving ground for formal verification capabilities in Zapata’s Quantum Pilot software platform. |
Rather than treating AI-generated quantum circuits as opaque black boxes, ForShor exposes a constrained policy-search interface governing how recursive Toom-Cook multiplication splits bit registers and schedules reversible additions, shifts, and negations. Any submitted policy that passes four kernel-checked conditions automatically inherits machine-checked proofs of Shor order-finding success probability and subcubic gate scaling, avoiding the need to re-prove surrounding modular exponentiation or continued-fraction logic.
By pairing machine-checked correctness with interactive proof maps, open documentation, and a community Discord, the partners aim to ensure that AI-discovered optimizations remain inspectable and reusable across broader fault-tolerant software stacks, including Zapata’s Quantum Pilot platform.
