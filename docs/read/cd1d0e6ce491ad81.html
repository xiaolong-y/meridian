Terra Quantum Publishes White Paper Detailing Post-Quantum Cryptography Migration Paths for Blockchain Ecosystems

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 10h quantum 313 words 2 min read

Deep-tech quantum company Terra Quantum AG has published a comprehensive white paper and companion research study analyzing the vulnerabilities of major blockchain platforms to cryptographically relevant quantum computers (CRQCs) and outlining post-quantum cryptography (PQC) migration frameworks.
The paper highlights that widely deployed public-key digital signature algorithms—including ECDSA (secp256k1) and Ed25519—are vulnerable to private key derivation via Shor’s algorithm. Recent quantum resource estimates indicate that breaking elliptic-curve discrete logarithms may require up to 20 times fewer physical qubits than previously projected, heightening urgency for decentralized networks, institutional custodians, and stablecoin issuers.

The analysis emphasizes that signature payload size is the primary technical bottleneck when integrating PQC into high-throughput ledgers. While finalized NIST standard ML-DSA-44 (Dilithium) generates 2,420-byte signatures (38x larger than Ed25519), the paper identifies FN-DSA (Falcon-512, under draft standard FIPS 206) as the most viable candidate for blockchain transaction layers due to its compact ≤666-byte signature footprint.
The publication also evaluates mitigation frameworks, including Solana’s application-level Winternitz One-Time Signature (WOTS) vaults, Algorand’s state proofs and native Falcon account roadmaps, and Ethereum’s EIP-8052 proposal for EVM-native Falcon-512 verification precompiles.
