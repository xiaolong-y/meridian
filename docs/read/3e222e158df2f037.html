Governor Newsom Signs Fusion Legislation and Allocates State Capital to Advance California Quantum Infrastructure

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 1d quantum 622 words 4 min read

California Governor Gavin Newsom has signed legislative measures and announced a $30 million joint budget allocation for quantum and deep-space research to advance state-level technology infrastructure. Announced at a quantum laboratory at UC Berkeley, the executive action expands the state’s Quantum California initiative by supporting university research hubs, public-private startup incubators, and hardware supply-chain infrastructure across physical sciences and semiconductor manufacturing.
The legislative package includes Senate Bill 925 (Sen. Jerry McNerney) and Assembly Bill 2647 (Asm. Lisa Calderon). SB 925 directs the California Energy Commission to establish a comprehensive strategic plan, certification protocols, and environmental review frameworks to commercialize fusion energy and scale advanced fusion manufacturing. Concurrently, AB 2647 mandates a statewide technical assessment regarding the integration of advanced nuclear technologies to support grid reliability toward California’s target of 100% zero-carbon electricity by 2045.
Complementing the executive announcement, Governor Newsom signed University of California-sponsored legislation (Senate Bill 895) placing the $7.5 billion California Science and Health Research Bond Act on the March 2028 state ballot. Designed to mitigate potential federal research funding fluctuations, the bond measure would fund public university capital projects, medical innovations, and emerging technology research.
A major infrastructure component within California’s quantum framework is the UCLA Quantum Innovation Hub (QIH) at the newly developed UCLA Research Park. QIH has secured global aerospace corporation Boeing as an industry partner to advance physical-layer quantum networking, entanglement distribution over optical fiber, and system design for space and defense communications—including an on-site entanglement-swapping testbed to model multi-node quantum network performance.
