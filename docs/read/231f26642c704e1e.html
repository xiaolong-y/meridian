Diraq Expands U.S. Footprint with New Mexico Quantum R&D Laboratory to Advance DARPA QBI Milestones

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 1d quantum 386 words 2 min read

Silicon spin-qubit hardware developer Diraq has executed a Memorandum of Understanding (MOU) with the New Mexico Technology and Innovation Office (TIO) and Roadrunner Venture Studios to establish a dedicated research and development laboratory in Albuquerque. Situated inside the newly opened Roadrunner Quantum Lab (RQL), the facility will house specialized measurement engineering teams focused on characterization, low-temperature testing, and device calibration for CMOS-compatible silicon quantum dots.
The state-backed expansion directly aligns with Diraq’s participation in the Defense Advanced Research Projects Agency’s Quantum Benchmarking Initiative (DARPA QBI), where Diraq is performing as an active Stage B contractor. State matching funds provided through New Mexico’s $450 million quantum technology allocation will directly finance the specialized cryogenic testing and characterization infrastructure at RQL. The Albuquerque lab will integrate sub-kelvin Maybell Quantum dilution refrigeration systems to characterize high-density silicon spin-qubit chips, complementing Diraq’s existing North American sites (Palo Alto, Chicago, Los Angeles) and primary R&D headquarters in Sydney, Australia.

By leveraging standard complementary metal-oxide-semiconductor (CMOS) fabrication processes, Diraq aims to achieve high-density qubit packing on standard silicon wafers, bypassing the footprint and interconnect bottlenecks that challenge non-semiconductor quantum platforms. The Albuquerque measurement laboratory will begin operational bring-up by the end of 2026, serving as a key testing node for verifying multi-qubit gate fidelities and control electronics required for utility-scale fault-tolerant quantum computing.
