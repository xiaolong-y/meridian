Moth Demonstrates Topology-Aware Quantum Data Encoding Across 120 Qubits Without SWAP Overhead

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 8h quantum 503 words 3 min read

Moth Quantum has introduced Tessa (Tessellated QPIXL), a hardware-native state-preparation scheme that encodes and retrieves up to 1,568 real-valued parameters across every qubit of an IBM Quantum processor without SWAP routing gates or ancilla qubits. Detailed in a technical preprint by Moth researcher Daniel Bultrini, the method partitions bipartite processor coupling graphs into interleaved address and data registers, overcoming the star-connectivity bottlenecks that have constrained prior angle-encoding protocols in quantum machine learning (QML).
| Tessa Hardware-Native Quantum Data Encoding Benchmarks | | |
|---|---|---|
| Benchmark Dimension | Processor & Encoding Mode | Circuit Cost & Measured Recovery |
| Square-Lattice (Single-Basis) | 120-qubit IBM Nighthawk r2 (ibm_phoenix, Z basis) | 784 values in 728 CZ gates (depth 23); 0.075 MAE and 0.96 correlation after contrast recovery. |
| Square-Lattice (Phase-Pair) | 120-qubit IBM Nighthawk r2 (ibm_phoenix, Z & X bases) | 1,568 values in 1,456 CZ gates (depth 43); 0.154 value MAE and 0.092 hue MAE at 8,192 shots/basis. |
| Heavy-Hex Architecture | 156-qubit IBM Heron r2 (ibm_fez, Z / Z & X bases) | 441 values (428 CZ gates, 0.95 correlation) in Z mode; 882 values (852 CZ gates) in pair mode. |
| Biological Sequences | Bloch-sphere constellation encoding (32,768 shots) | Recovered 92.3% of 784-nt MT-CO3 DNA sequence and 71.5% of 393-aa p53 protein sequence. |
Conventional amplitude and angle-encoding algorithms such as QPIXL and QCrank rely on uniformly controlled rotations that require all-to-all star connectivity between shared address qubits and target data qubits, forcing deep SWAP chains on nearest-neighbor superconducting chips. Tessa eliminates routing overhead by 2-coloring the physical device graph so each data qubit executes a multiplexer controlled strictly by its adjacent address neighbors, yielding a circuit depth governed by local vertex degree rather than total chip size.
Evaluated without quantum error mitigation on both heavy-hex and square-lattice topologies, the scheme surpassed the 128-value hardware record previously set by QCrank for arbitrary real-valued vectors, demonstrating image, audio wavetable, MIDI, and genetic sequence reconstruction using lightweight statistical dynamic-range recovery. The author notes that because readout shots divide across the 2m local address states of a degree-m data qubit, sampling overhead scales exponentially with local connectivity, positioning Tessa for low-degree QML feature ingestion rather than general-purpose classical memory.
Alongside the research release, Moth has made Tessa available inside Atlas, its alpha-stage creative and developer platform serving thousands of early users across music, gaming, and visual media. Accessible through both no-code web engines and programmatic APIs targeting quantum hardware backends and emulators, Atlas pairs Tessa Image processing with a suite of quantum-driven creative utilities including a quantum audio re-encoder, MIDI variation generator, Quantum Blur, Entanglement Shader, Telablur image morphing, and hardware-seeded labyrinth maze generation.
