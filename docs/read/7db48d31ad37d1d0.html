RIKEN and Quantinuum Demonstrate Heterogeneous Multi-QPU Workflows on Fugaku

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 5h quantum 665 words 4 min read

Researchers from Quantinuum, the University of Tsukuba, and the RIKEN Center for Computational Science (R-CCS) have demonstrated a hybrid computing architecture linking the supercomputer Fugaku simultaneously with two distinct physical quantum processors: the Quantinuum Reimei trapped-ion system and the ibm_kobe superconducting system. Detailed in an IEEE conference paper for SC26 titled “Quantum-HPC Workflows Across Multiple Quantum Computing Platforms: Two Case Studies,” the team adapted Quantinuum’s open-source Tierkreis workflow management system to native supercomputing batch environments, executing the first demonstrated multi-modality quantum-classical workflow across geographically separated QPUs.

Hybrid quantum-classical algorithms often depend on fragmented glue code, bash scripts, and manual job handling that fail to scale within production supercomputing environments. To formalize multi-system orchestration, the authors extended Tierkreis—a dataflow framework that models computational graphs as directed acyclic structures—by introducing specialized executors for Fugaku’s native job scheduler (pjsub) and direct quantum submission channels (sqcsub). These interfaces bypass commercial cloud queues, enabling Fugaku compute and login nodes to dispatch tasks to quantum coprocessors via low-latency gRPC services.
The primary demonstration evaluated a modality-aware algorithm combining the distinct strengths of superconducting and trapped-ion hardware to compute the ground-state energy of a 16-site transverse-field Ising model. Rather than forcing a single architecture to execute the entire routine, the workflow separated the task across platforms:
- Iterative State Optimization on ibm_kobe: The Variational Quantum Eigensolver (VQE) requires rapid iteration over thousands of circuit evaluations. The workflow executed 500 iterations of a parameterized Hamiltonian variational ansatz using the SPSA optimizer on the 156-qubit ibm_kobe processor, collecting 20,000 shots per circuit to find an approximate ground-state representation.
- High-Fidelity Phase Estimation on Reimei: Because Quantum Phase Estimation (QPE) requires deep coherent circuits that challenge noisy systems, the parameters derived from ibm_kobe were transferred classically to seed the initial state for an Information Theory QPE algorithm on Reimei. Executed across 16 system qubits, 1 ancilla, and 3 scratch qubits dedicated to hardware leakage-detection gadgets, Reimei performed Trotterized time-evolution using 300 shots.
On physical hardware, using the fast superconducting processor to “warm-start” the trapped-ion phase estimation routine reduced the energy error to (E − Eexact)/J = 0.61 ± 0.36, substantially outperforming standalone VQE on ibm_kobe (2.47 ± 0.10) and a classically optimized product-state baseline (3.45). A secondary case study re-executed Fugaku-driven biomolecular excited-state workflows (subtractive ONIOM and TE-QSCI), validating that Tierkreis automated 80% of runtime coordination across classical chemistry packages (NTChem) and quantum circuit generation with line-level backend reconfigurability.
The work demonstrates that complex quantum chemistry and condensed-matter workflows can operate across heterogeneous quantum hardware architectures without physical quantum interconnects, using classical supercomputers as central orchestrators.
