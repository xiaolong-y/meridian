Monarch Quantum Supplies Integrated Photonics for Next Phase of NASA and Infleqtion’s Space-Based Gravity Gradiometer

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 9h quantum 438 words 3 min read

San Diego-based quantum optics firm Monarch Quantum has detailed its role providing integrated photonics hardware to support Infleqtion under NASA’s Quantum Gravity Gradiometer Pathfinder (QGGPf) mission. The announcement follows NASA’s recent $20 million follow-on award to Infleqtion, bringing total agency commitments for the pathfinder program to $40 million as hardware transitions toward a planned 2030 orbital demonstration.
Led by NASA’s Jet Propulsion Laboratory (JPL) in Southern California, the QGGPf mission aims to deploy the world’s first space-based Earth quantum gravity gradient sensor in low-Earth orbit. Building on NASA’s satellite-to-satellite tracking heritage (GRACE and GRACE-FO), the pathfinder will measure minute variations in Earth’s gravitational acceleration using ultracold rubidium atoms to track groundwater depletion, ice-sheet mass loss, and subsurface geophysical shifts directly within a single instrument.

Ruggedizing Atomic Physics for Spaceflight Environments
A primary hurdle in transitioning cold-atom quantum sensing from laboratory environments to spaceflight is managing optical complexity under severe Size, Weight, and Power (SWaP) constraints. Atom interferometry requires precise, narrow-linewidth laser light to trap, cool, and interrogate atoms without beam drift caused by launch vibration or thermal fluctuations in orbit.
Monarch Quantum supplies compact photonics subsystems that integrate lasers, micro-optics, fiber routing, and control electronics into ruggedized enclosures. By offloading photonics packaging and environmental stabilization to specialized component providers, sensor integrators like Infleqtion can focus on core atomic physics, magnetic shielding, and detector electronics.
Infleqtion—which has supported the Cold Atom Lab (CAL) aboard the International Space Station since 2018—is preparing to validate the engineering development unit at the Einstein-Elevator drop-tower facility in Hannover, Germany, ahead of final payload qualification for spaceflight.
