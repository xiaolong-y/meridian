University of Amsterdam Awards €800,000 Across Four Quantum Simulation Projects for Chemistry and Materials Design

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 22h quantum 363 words 2 min read

The University of Amsterdam (UvA) Faculty of Science has launched four two-year research projects under its new Q-SIM4MMD initiative, committing €200,000 in seed funding to each team (€800,000 ($877K USD) total) to evaluate quantum simulation and quantum machine learning for molecular and materials discovery. Embedded within UvA’s Molecular and Material Design Technology Hub (MMD TechHub), the effort is supported by a newly formed Q-Sim expertise group connecting researchers across physics, chemistry, life sciences, and computer science with the QuSoft research center and Quantum Delta NL Amsterdam.
UvA Q-SIM4MMD Funded Quantum Simulation Projects (€200,000 Each Over Two Years)
Project Title
Principal Investigators
Technical Focus & Application Targets
Quantum Machine Learning for Molecular and Material Design
Alberto Pérez de Alba Ortíz, Clélia de Mulatier (UvA)
Quantum-circuit kernels for Bayesian optimization modeling PFAS and nanoplastic binding to human proteins.
Exploring Quantum Computing in Bioinformatics
Aalt-Jan van Dijk, Anna Heintz-Buschart (UvA)
Benchmarking hybrid quantum-classical workflows against classical tools for enzyme specificity and microbiome DNA analysis.
Quantum-assisted topological molecular representations for screening large chemical databases.
Testing Quantum Simulations Against Established Methods
Philippe Corboz, Arghavan Safavi Naini (UvA), Natalia Chepiga (Oxford)
Benchmarking quantum simulations of 2D Hubbard models and spin-chain dynamics against classical tensor networks.
Launched in June 2024 across eight Faculty of Science institutes, the MMD TechHub will assist the four project teams in transitioning proof-of-concept algorithms toward external public-private partnerships and follow-up industrial funding. By pairing domain chemists and biologists directly with quantum algorithm developers, the initiative aims to identify specific computational bottlenecks where near-term quantum processors or quantum-inspired methods can complement established classical modeling pipelines.
