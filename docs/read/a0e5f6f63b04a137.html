IBM, Fermilab, and NVIDIA Unveil Modular Quantum Co-Design Platform, Slurm HRES Integration, and GPU QEC Tools at SC26

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 19h quantum 636 words 4 min read

At the SC26 supercomputing conference in Chicago, IBM, NVIDIA, and the U.S. Department of Energy’s Fermi National Accelerator Laboratory (Fermilab) announced plans for a modular quantum-classical co-design platform, accompanied by native quantum resource scheduling in Slurm 26.11 and new GPU-accelerated quantum error-correction (QEC) software releases.

Fermilab, IBM, and NVIDIA Plan Modular Co-Design Platform
Building on the long-running collaboration between IBM and Fermilab’s Superconducting Quantum Materials and Systems (SQMS) Center, the three organizations will physically co-locate NVIDIA DGX B200 systems with IBM Quantum Nighthawk processors. Fermilab will coordinate researcher access and hybrid workflows through its HEPCloud infrastructure to study lattice gauge theories, neutrino interactions, and nuclear physics simulations.
In its initial phase, the testbed will emulate inter-module communication links to quantify how routing errors, latency, and classical feedback affect multi-QPU execution. A subsequent phase plans to integrate Fermilab’s ultracoherent superconducting radio-frequency (SRF) cavity hardware as quantum memories, entanglement buffers, and bosonic error-correction resources, complementing the IBM-ORNL-AMD co-design platform deployed earlier this month in Poughkeepsie.
GPU-Accelerated QEC Decoders and Runtime Frameworks
Detailed in a technical study by NVIDIA researchers, the new TCBP-JOBS algorithm pairs Trajectory-Calibrated Belief Propagation (TCBP) with GPU-parallel Jittered Ordered Statistics Decoding Beam Search (JOBS) across surface, color, and bivariate-bicycle quantum low-density parity-check (qLDPC) codes. At physical error rates between 0.1% and 0.4%, TCBP-JOBS maintains millisecond-scale serial latencies (4.08 ms for 12-round gross-code memory and 13.5 ms for logical lattice surgery at 0.2% error) suitable for neutral-atom and trapped-ion feed-forward operations, while exact search decoders such as Tesseract remain more accurate in ultra-low-noise regimes (0.01% to 0.05%).
Released inside CUDA-Q QEC 0.9.0 alongside a containerized Decoding Server, composable parallel-window task graphs, and 1.3x to 3.2x faster Relay-BP kernels, TCBP-JOBS joins broader SC26 QEC tooling including IBM and AMD’s open-source Pelagos GPU simulation suite, a multi-institutional hierarchical decoding taxonomy co-authored by AMD, HPE, Riverlane, Xanadu, IQM, and IBM, and an NVQLink FPGA control bridge demonstrated by Altera and Oxford Quantum Circuits.
Native Multi-Node QPU Scheduling in Slurm 26.11
To eliminate custom middleware at the roughly 30 supercomputing centers deploying more than 50 quantum processors worldwide, NVIDIA, IBM, and Pasqal integrated the open-source Quantum Resource Management Interface (QRMI) and Quantum Device Management Interface (QDMI) directly into Slurm 26.11’s Hierarchical Resources (HRES) scheduler. Because QPUs operate as shared multi-node resources rather than node-local accelerators, HRES allows administrators to allocate, reserve, and account for quantum hardware alongside CPUs and GPUs under standard cluster policies.
