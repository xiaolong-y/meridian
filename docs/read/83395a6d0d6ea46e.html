Horizon Quantum Announces Q3 2026 Financial Results: Advances Triple Alpha Pulse Integration with OQC and Announces Intent to Acquire Entropica Labs

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 6h quantum 794 words 4 min read

Horizon Quantum Holdings Ltd. (Nasdaq: HQ), a developer of hardware-agnostic software infrastructure for quantum computing, has announced its financial results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026. The Singapore-headquartered company highlighted milestones in low-level processor control with Oxford Quantum Circuits (OQC), the implementation of scalable multi-qubit characterization techniques, and a planned acquisition to expand its capabilities in quantum error correction.

Financial Highlights & Capital Position
Horizon Quantum is primarily in the research, development, and partner-validation phase for its software platforms, recording no commercial revenue during Q3 2026. Operating expenses for the third quarter totaled $10.49 million, compared to $4.87 million in Q3 2025. The 115.5% increase was driven by:
- Research & Development: Rose to $4.46 million (up from $2.01M in Q3 2025), primarily reflecting expanded technical engineering headcount, salary adjustments, and computing infrastructure costs. Non-cash share-based compensation within R&D accounted for $1.71 million of the total.
- General & Administrative: Rose to $5.22 million (compared to $2.30M in Q3 2025), driven by public company operating expenses, professional advisory fees, and $2.02 million in share-based compensation.
- Selling & Marketing: Remained steady at $0.43 million.
GAAP Operating Loss for Q3 2026 was ($10.49) million. Below the operating line, Horizon reported GAAP Net Income of $33.41 million ($0.61 per basic ordinary share), compared to a net loss of ($5.19) million in Q3 2025. This GAAP profit resulted entirely from a $43.10 million non-cash accounting gain recognized on the quarterly fair-value remeasurement of warrant-related derivative liabilities following a decline in the company’s share price during the quarter.
On a non-GAAP basis, excluding share-based compensation, transaction-related items, and derivative fair-value adjustments, Adjusted EBITDA loss was ($6.34) million for Q3 2026, compared to an Adjusted EBITDA loss of ($3.01) million in Q3 2025. The company ended the period with $102.79 million in cash and cash equivalents and zero long-term debt.
Hardware Ecosystem & Software Advancements
- Pulse-Level QPU Integration with OQC: Completed pulse-level integration between Horizon’s Triple Alpha integrated development environment (IDE) and Oxford Quantum Circuits’ (OQC) 32-qubit superconducting processor, Toshiko. Using Triple Alpha’s pulse control layer, engineers accessed control electronics to measure coherence parameters (T1 relaxation and T2 dephasing) and executed calibrated gate routines directly through high-level programming code.
- Multiplexed Characterization on 100+ Qubits: Developed and validated a proprietary multiplexed characterization protocol on quantum processors exceeding 100 qubits. The protocol measures ZZ crosstalk and control crosstalk concurrently across multiple qubits. On a 100-qubit processor, this technique reduced the number of characterization experiments from approximately 5,000 down to 7—a greater than 700-fold reduction in characterization overhead compared to conventional pairwise testing.
- Triple Alpha Runtime Architecture: Progressed the deployment of Triple Alpha as an abstraction layer for quantum execution. The software enables dynamic memory management, control flow logic (if/else and loops), and mid-circuit measurement integration across heterogeneous QPU backends.
Strategic M&A: Planned Acquisition of Entropica Labs
- Entropica Labs LOI: Signed a non-binding letter of intent (LOI) to acquire Entropica Labs Pte. Ltd., a Singapore-based quantum software developer specializing in quantum error correction (QEC) and fault-tolerant architecture tools.
- Deal Structure: The anticipated transaction consideration is valued at $27.0 million, payable in Horizon Quantum Class A ordinary shares, subject to due diligence, definitive agreements, and closing conditions. The transaction is designed to bring dedicated error-correction algorithms directly into Horizon’s compilation and deployment stack.
