Harvard, QuEra, NVIDIA, Anthropic, and Three National Labs Secure $12 Million DOE Award for AI-Driven Quantum Error Correction

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 8h quantum 411 words 3 min read

QuEra Computing has joined a Harvard University-led consortium awarded $12 million by the U.S. Department of Energy (DOE) to develop artificial intelligence-designed quantum error correction tailored to scientific computing workloads. Titled Application-Aware Error Correction Codesign for Scientific Quantum Computing (ASQC), the initiative is one of 12 Phase II awards under the DOE’s Genesis Mission administered by the Advanced Scientific Computing Research (ASCR) division, uniting researchers from Harvard, MIT, Cornell University, Los Alamos National Laboratory, Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory, Sandia National Laboratories, QuEra, NVIDIA, and Anthropic.
| DOE ASQC Consortium Technical Workstreams & Partner Contributions | | |
|---|---|---|
| Workstream / Dimension | Technical Scope | Participating Partners & Targets |
| Error-Sensitivity & Code Metrics | Train AI models to identify dominant fault pathways and define verifiable application-level code quality metrics. | Quantum simulation, materials science, chemistry, and nuclear science workloads. |
| AI Code, Decoder & Compiler Design | Co-design application-tailored QEC codes, fast neural decoders, and reinforcement-learning atom-shuttling compilers. | Builds on neural decoders (up to 17× error reduction), Cornell’s RL compiler, and LLM circuit discovery with Anthropic. |
| Hardware & Application Validation | Benchmark adaptive QEC pipelines and hybrid quantum-classical strongly correlated electron simulations. | Tested on QuEra neutral-atom processors (supporting the 2028 Libra roadmap) and lab testbeds at Harvard and MIT. |
Led by Harvard physicist and QuEra co-founder Mikhail Lukin, ASQC aims to reduce the physical qubit and gate overhead required for fault tolerance by matching error-correcting codes, decoders, and compilers to the structure of specific scientific applications rather than treating all noise uniformly. Within the consortium, Eun-Ah Kim’s group at Cornell University will develop a reinforcement-learning compiler to translate quantum algorithms into atom movements and gate sequences, establish quantitative benchmarks for AI code search, and test hybrid quantum-classical simulations of strongly interacting electrons in quantum materials.
The program builds on recent collaborations across the team that demonstrated neural-network decoders reducing logical error rates by up to 17×, large language model-driven quantum circuit discovery, and autonomous laser-locking routines developed with Anthropic’s Claude that restored drifted optical locks in 695 of 700 testbed trials. Experimental validation on neutral-atom hardware at QuEra, Harvard, and MIT will directly inform QuEra’s development of its Libra fault-tolerant processor planned for Amazon Braket in 2028.
