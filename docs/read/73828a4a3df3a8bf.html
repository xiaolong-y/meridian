IDEMIA and KT Deploy Quantum-Resistant SIMs Supporting Dual NIST and KpqC Algorithms for South Korean Military Networks

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 57m quantum 603 words 4 min read

Security and identity technology vendor IDEMIA Secure Transactions (IST) has partnered with South Korean telecommunications operator KT Corporation to implement post-quantum cryptographic primitives on smart-card and SIM platforms for national defense communications. The collaboration operates under South Korea’s 2026 Post-Quantum Cryptography Pilot Conversion Project, organized by the Ministry of Science and ICT (MSIT) and the Korea Internet & Security Agency (KISA).
The pilot represents the first reported deployment of domestic Korean Post-Quantum Cryptography (KpqC) algorithms alongside international NIST standards directly within a tamper-resistant SIM/eSIM secure element. By serving as an isolated hardware root of trust, the Quantum-Resistant SIM offloads processing- and memory-intensive lattice-based cryptographic handshakes from host devices, allowing South Korea’s defense forces to achieve crypto-agility without replacing tactical endpoints.

Embedded Crypto-Agility at the Secure Element Layer
Post-quantum cryptographic algorithms introduce significantly larger key sizes, signatures, and memory demands compared to legacy asymmetric schemes like RSA and ECC. Running these algorithms directly on low-power internet-of-things (IoT) devices, tactical sensors, and field terminals can introduce substantial processing latency and drain battery life.
IDEMIA’s approach embeds the cryptographic operations inside a dedicated, tamper-resistant smart-card platform. In this configuration, the SIM generates, stores, and executes quantum-safe session keys internally, insulating sensitive key material from host system vulnerabilities. By packaging the cryptographic runtime within standard SIM/eSIM sockets, military operators can upgrade fielded commercial hardware to post-quantum standards by swapping or re-provisioning the secure module.
A primary objective of the MSIT/KISA pilot is testing crypto-agility—the technical capacity to alternate between cryptographic standards without rewriting application software. Supporting both NIST standards and the domestic KpqC framework within a unified secure element ensures interoperability with multinational allied forces (such as the U.S. and NATO) while remaining compliant with South Korea’s national sovereign cryptography mandates.
Validating Operational Resilience Across Military Infrastructure
Conducted on behalf of South Korea’s Ministry of National Defense and the Army Information and Communication School, KT’s pilot evaluates PQC encryption and authentication under operational military conditions. Target platforms include:
- Uncrewed Systems: Securing telemetry, flight commands, and mission payload links between tactical uncrewed aerial vehicles (UAVs) and ground control stations.
- Base Surveillance & Video Storage: Implementing quantum-safe authenticated encryption across perimeter CCTV feeds and digital video evidence archives.
- Defense 5G Networks: Testing post-quantum mutual authentication across core private 5G military wireless base stations and connected endpoints.
The program serves as a reference model for South Korea’s broader timeline to migrate critical national infrastructure away from quantum-vulnerable public-key algorithms over the next decade.
