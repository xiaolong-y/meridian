Qunova Computing Achieves Chemical Accuracy at 80 Qubits on Iron-Sulfur Cluster Using IBM Quantum System Two and Fugaku

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 1d quantum 401 words 3 min read

South Korean quantum algorithm developer Qunova Computing has demonstrated chemical accuracy in an 80-qubit electronic-structure simulation of the [4Fe-4S] iron-sulfur cubane cluster, executing a hybrid workflow across an IBM Quantum System Two processor and the Fugaku supercomputer at RIKEN. Running its Handover Iterative Variational Quantum Eigensolver (HI-VQE) algorithm, the team achieved an extrapolated ground-state energy within 1.6 milliHartree (1 kcal/mol) of classical density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG) benchmarks while cutting classical supercomputing costs by roughly 300× compared to prior quantum diagonalization runs.

Transition-metal complexes such as iron-sulfur clusters govern critical biological and industrial processes, including nitrogen fixation and clean hydrogen synthesis, yet their strongly correlated electrons challenge both standard density functional theory and early quantum algorithms. While prior quantum-centric supercomputing experiments demonstrated subspace sampling on smaller active spaces, reaching quantitative chemical accuracy at 80 qubits has remained an open milestone.
Qunova’s HI-VQE architecture couples quantum state sampling with classical subspace diagonalization without relying on linearity or spatial locality assumptions, enabling application across catalysts, battery electrodes, and pharmaceutical targets. The company plans to integrate orbital rotation and optimization routines into future releases to process between 500 million and 3 billion unique electronic configurations.
