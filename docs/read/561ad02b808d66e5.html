TOYO Corporation Acquires Second On-Premises IQM System to Open Dual-QPU Testbed for Japan’s Ecosystem

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 1d quantum 351 words 2 min read

Japanese measurement solutions provider TOYO Corporation has expanded its quantum hardware commitments by purchasing an IQM Spark (a 5-qubit educational and algorithm verification platform) from IQM Quantum Computers (NASDAQ: IQMX). The acquisition follows TOYO’s earlier order of a 20-qubit IQM Radiance processor. Both systems are scheduled for physical delivery by late 2026 and are projected to reach full operational status in early 2027.
Under the dual-deployment model, TOYO will install the IQM Spark at its R&D center in Kiba, Tokyo, dedicated to talent development, hardware component benchmarking, and algorithm verification. The 20-qubit IQM Radiance will operate at the Global Research and Development Center for Business by Quantum-AI technology (G-QuAT)—a facility managed by Japan’s National Institute of Advanced Industrial Science and Technology (AIST). By interconnecting both platforms, TOYO will provide open on-premises and cloud access to Japanese universities, startups, and industrial researchers, aligning with national strategic targets of 10 million domestic quantum users and ¥50 trillion in quantum-generated economic value by 2030.

The transaction expands IQM’s installed base in the Asia-Pacific region, adding to systems previously deployed in South Korea and Taiwan. Commercial progress follows IQM’s public listing on the Nasdaq Global Select Market and an order backlog exceeding €102 million across 26 full-stack systems sold globally.
