U.S. Quantum Primacy Commission Outlines Industrial Strategy to Counter Global Supply-Chain Risks

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 4h quantum 460 words 3 min read

The Commission on U.S. Quantum Primacy (CUSP), an advisory coalition convened by the Special Competitive Studies Project (SCSP), has published its preliminary strategy report, Defining the American Quantum Era. Co-chaired by U.S. Senators Todd Young (R-IN) and Ben Ray Luján (D-NM) alongside SCSP President Ylli Bajraktari, the report warns that while the United States maintains an advantage in fundamental research and operational cloud platforms, severe domestic disparities in cleanroom capital costs, cryogenic supply chains, and annual public funding threaten long-term commercial competitiveness.
| CUSP Strategic Roadmap: Four Pillars of U.S. Quantum Primacy | | |
|---|---|---|
| Strategic Pillar | Recommended Programmatic Mechanism | Industrial & Operational Objective |
| Discovery | Dedicated Algorithm & Foundational Research Tracks | Stabilizes multi-year agency funding and creates open-access programs to discover novel mathematical primitives and algorithmic families. |
| Development | ECP-Style Co-Design & NIST Interoperability Effort | Funds concurrent hardware-software engineering modeled after DOE’s Exascale Computing Project; standardizes multi-QPU benchmarks with industry. |
| Deployment | DOE Grand Challenges & Accelerated PQC Migration | Establishes repeatable hybrid quantum-classical workflows while transitioning critical civilian infrastructure to post-quantum cryptography. |
| Scale | National Quantum Foundry Access Program & DPA Title III | Provides shared microfabrication and packaging facilities to offset high cleanroom buildout costs; secures domestic cryogenics and helium-3 supply lines. |
The report defines quantum primacy as the enduring capability to discover, engineer, manufacture, and field quantum systems across national security and commercial markets. Drawing on data from 2021 through 2025, the commission highlights a growing industrial imbalance: while U.S. entities operated an estimated 39 to 73 deployed quantum computers in 2025 compared to China’s 15 to 18, China’s estimated public funding of $3 billion annually outpaces the U.S. federal commitment of approximately $1 billion. Furthermore, domestic fabrication faces acute economic pressure, with U.S. cleanroom construction costs averaging $800 to $1,000 per square foot compared to approximately $150 per square foot in China.
Supply chain concentration represents a primary vulnerability. The report notes that following international export controls, domestic dilution refrigerator production within China expanded roughly 58% between 2021 and 2025, compared to 35% growth in the United States. To address these vulnerabilities, the commission recommends federal intervention to safeguard specialized radio-frequency electronics, optical modulators, and cryogenic isotopes before production bottlenecks restrict commercial scale.
The commission comprises executive leadership from IBM, Google Quantum AI, IonQ, SandboxAQ, DARPA, Los Alamos National Laboratory, Sandia National Laboratories, and the University of Maryland. A comprehensive final report detailing specific legislative provisions, workforce initiatives, and budget proposals is scheduled for publication in Spring 2027.
