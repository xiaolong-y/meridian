Chattanooga Establishes Nation’s First Quantum Pre-Apprenticeship Program

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 7h quantum 377 words 2 min read

The Chattanooga Quantum Collaborative (CQC) and BuildWithin have launched the first quantum pre-apprenticeship program in the United States, a federally aligned initiative designed to integrate quantum readiness into the commercial workforce. Operating from June 29 through September 18, 2026, the 12-week program follows the U.S. Department of Labor’s apprenticeship framework. Supported by a National Science Foundation grant awarded to the University of Tennessee at Chattanooga (UTC) Research Institute, the inaugural cohort of 10 participants will receive a $2,500 stipend upon completion of the curriculum delivered via BuildWithin’s workforce technology platform.
Unlike traditional academic programs focused on theoretical physics or doctoral research, this pre-apprenticeship targets mid-career professionals with existing technical baselines, such as IT specialists, operations leaders, and analysts. The curriculum is designed to be accessible to those without advanced degrees in mathematics, focusing instead on practical applications within sectors like logistics, healthcare, and energy. By emphasizing “intrapreneurship,” the program aims to equip workers already embedded in organizations with the skills necessary to identify and lead quantum adoption strategies, avoiding the talent bottlenecks that previously slowed enterprise cloud and AI migrations.
The hybrid curriculum combines asynchronous digital coursework with live industry speaker sessions and mentorship from “Quantum Learning Guides.” Participants will gain hands-on exposure to tools and content informed by industry leaders and federal agencies, including IBM, IonQ, D-Wave, and the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST). As a capstone project, each participant is required to develop a specific quantum use case tailored to their current employer’s operational needs, ensuring the training translates directly into organizational value and localized economic mobility.
The launch solidifies Chattanooga’s position as a regional hub for quantum infrastructure, which already includes a commercially available quantum network and a quantum computer currently coming online. By anchoring this program in the proven Department of Labor infrastructure used for over 1,000 other occupations, the CQC and BuildWithin have created a replicable national model for quantum workforce development. This “Peopleware” approach ensures that as quantum hardware matures, the workforce is prepared to manage real-world deployments across the “Quantum Prairie” and beyond.
