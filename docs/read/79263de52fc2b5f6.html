NowSecure Benchmark Finds 97% of Mobile Applications Lack Post-Quantum Cryptography Ahead of Mandated Timelines

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 1d quantum 656 words 4 min read

Automated mobile application security testing specialist NowSecure has published benchmark threat intelligence assessing the post-quantum readiness of the global mobile software ecosystem. Evaluating 136,711 unique iOS and Android applications over a 90-day window ending October 5, 2026, the study revealed that 96.9% (rounded to 97%) of assessed apps show no detectable Post-Quantum Cryptography (PQC) code or libraries in their binary builds.
The findings spotlight a critical blind spot in enterprise cryptographic migration: while security operations teams increasingly catalog server certificates and core web backbones, mobile endpoints handling logins, payments, and encrypted communications are routinely excluded from cryptographic inventories. Because mobile traffic frequently traverses untrusted public cellular and Wi-Fi networks, mobile apps are acutely vulnerable to “Harvest Now, Decrypt Later” (HNDL) data-collection campaigns targeting sensitive communications for retroactive decryption once a Cryptographically Relevant Quantum Computer (CRQC) emerges.

The “Mobile Gap”: Why Source Scans Miss Asymmetric Vulnerabilities
Most commercial mobile applications secure user authentication, transaction signing, and TLS sessions using classical public-key cryptography—primarily RSA and Elliptic Curve Cryptography (ECC)—which are vulnerable to Shor’s algorithm running on future quantum hardware. While NIST standardized its core quantum-resistant algorithms in August 2024 (FIPS 203 ML-KEM, FIPS 204 ML-DSA, and FIPS 205 SLH-DSA), practical implementation across mobile ecosystems has stalled.
A central driver of this lag is software architecture. A significant proportion of mobile encryption is governed by third-party Software Development Kits (SDKs) and compiled libraries—such as analytics, payment gateways, and ad trackers—which developers do not author directly. Traditional static Application Security Testing (SAST) and Software Bill of Materials (SBOM) dependency scans inspect source repositories but frequently overlook the compiled cryptography shipped in final store-ready binaries.
Compounding this exposure, runtime testing revealed that more than 3,100 mobile apps still utilize obsolete ciphers like DES and RC4, highlighting that many organizations must resolve basic cryptographic hygiene while simultaneously planning for quantum-safe crypto-agility.
Regulatory Alignment and the Two-Year Migration Runway
The research emphasizes that organizations face fixed compliance horizons. Under U.S. Executive Order 14412, federal agencies are mandated to transition high-value systems to quantum-safe key establishment by December 31, 2030, followed by digital signatures by December 31, 2031. Concurrently, Google has established 2029 as its internal PQC migration objective, and Gartner warns that CISOs who delay PQC planning risk compounding remediation expenses as Q-Day nears.
Federal audit data released by the Government Accountability Office (GAO) in October 2026 (GAO-27-108740) reported that none of 24 major federal agencies have fully implemented essential PQC preparation practices, citing resource and inventory bottlenecks. NowSecure recommends that enterprise AppSec leads establish a structured two-year migration cycle: cataloging compiled binaries (both first-party and vendor-supplied), auditing third-party SDK roadmaps, and retesting cryptographic runtime behaviors on physical devices ahead of 2028 deployment milestones.
