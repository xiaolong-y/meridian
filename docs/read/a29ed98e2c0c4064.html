All Nine Award Winners in Japan’s NEDO Quantum Challenge Leverage IBM Hardware; Four Dedicated Industry Sponsor Prizes Awarded

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 18h quantum 929 words 5 min read

Japan’s New Energy and Industrial Technology Development Organization (NEDO), operating under the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI), has formally announced the award recipients for Season 1 of its national prize-based competition, the “NEDO Prize-Funded Program / Development of Social Problem Solutions Using Quantum Computing.“ The award ceremony was held at the Marunouchi Building Hall in Tokyo following the conclusion of research evaluations across 87 competitive entries.
The program aims to accelerate the transition of quantum algorithms from theoretical concepts to practical industrial implementation. Out of 87 project submissions—spanning 46 in Society 5.0, 25 in Quality of Life (QoL), and 16 in Cool Japan—judges awarded nine general prizes, while also presenting dedicated corporate sponsor awards via the private-sector-led WIRED Quantum Partnership (sponsored by Quantinuum, Mitsui & Co., Mitsubishi Estate, and Toshiba). All nine general track award winners conducted their research on IBM Quantum hardware, with six relying exclusively on IBM superconducting systems.

Rigorous Benchmark Standards and Transition to Season 2 Commercialization
The judging committee, chaired by Professor Keisuke Fujii of Kyoto University, held the evaluation criteria to strict computational standards. Consequently, the committee declined to award first-place prizes in both Society 5.0 and Cool Japan, and withheld the Jury Chairman’s Special Award, setting a high threshold for projects claiming verifiable advantages over classical supercomputing.
Across the challenge, 65 of the 80 participating teams (81%) selected IBM Quantum Platform systems, logging 11,159 execution jobs and utilizing over 5,900 minutes of QPU compute time. IBM Fellow and Director of IBM Research Jay Gambetta emphasized that participant success was accelerated by coupling hardware with third-party software catalog extensions, citing the direct deployment of Q-CTRL’s error-mitigation tooling and Kipu Quantum’s Iskay algorithm via Qiskit Functions.
During the ceremony, special lectures were delivered by Makoto Gonokami, President of RIKEN, and Mitsunobu Koshiba, Chairman of the Quantum Innovation Initiative (QII) Council. To ensure long-term commercialization beyond the prize awards, NEDO announced a series of post-challenge support mechanisms:
- Case Study Release (December 2026): NEDO will publish a comprehensive “Collection of Case Studies on Understanding and Connecting with Quantum Technology“ to profile industrial problem definitions, technical architectures, and commercialization plans for partner matching.
- R&D Barrier Analysis (March 2027): A structured analysis report titled “Quantum R&D: The Common Sense of the Future” will synthesize operational lessons and technical bottlenecks identified across Season 1 participants.
- Launch of Season 2 (2026–2027): Research operations for Season 2 are already underway, focusing on expanding startup incubation, business model planning, and corporate technology transfer.
