NVIDIA and Jülich Run 104-Qubit Magic State Cultivation Simulation on JUPITER Supercomputer

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 13h quantum 467 words 3 min read

In a technical milestone announced at GTC Berlin 2026, NVIDIA and the Jülich Supercomputing Centre (JSC) at Forschungszentrum Jülich have executed a 104-qubit fault-tolerant quantum computing simulation on JUPITER, Europe’s first exascale supercomputer.
Detailed in an official release and accompanying NVIDIA technical report (“Advancing Fault-Tolerant Quantum Computing with GPU-Accelerated Simulation“), the simulation modeled the physical execution of magic state cultivation under realistic hardware noise—validating a fault-tolerant protocol that reduces physical qubit overhead by up to 90% compared to traditional cascade distillation factories.

Exascale Simulation Mechanics & Qutrit Encoding
Simulating magic state cultivation requires tracking non-Clifford operations and non-Markovian noise that cannot be simplified by stabilizer tableau methods (such as the Gottesman-Knill theorem):
- Qutrit Leakage Encoding: To capture physical superconducting qubit errors where state population escapes the computational subspace (|0⟩, |1⟩) into a third energy level (|2⟩), the team packed groups of three-level qutrit systems into binary registers (e.g., 5 qutrits require 243 basis states, packed into 8 binary bits).
- Memory & Scale: The 45-bit encoded state vector tracked 35.2 trillion complex amplitudes, consuming 512 TiB of aggregate GPU memory (64 GiB per GH200 node) across 8,192 GPUs on JUPITER.
- Development Pathway at NVAQC: Development, verification, and code scaling were conducted at the newly established NVIDIA Accelerated Quantum Research Center (NVAQC) using GB200 NVL72 architectures, reducing simulation development timelines from weeks to days before launching on JUPITER.
The exascale simulation directly complements Google Quantum AI’s foundational cultivation theory, builds upon Google’s Willow hardware cultivation demonstration in Science, and expands NVIDIA’s CUDA-Q platform momentum across European supercomputing hubs.
