Aalborg University Orders ConScience Plug’N’Teach Superconducting Quantum Computer for Hands-On Engineering Training

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 12h quantum 302 words 2 min read

Aalborg University (AAU) in Denmark has selected Swedish quantum hardware company ConScience AB to supply an on-premises superconducting quantum computer for hands-on engineering education and component research. Scheduled for installation at AAU in 2027, the Plug’N’Teach system will launch with two superconducting qubits before expanding to seven qubits.
Occupying two rooms at Aalborg University’s Department of Electronic Systems, the full-stack educational platform integrates ConScience’s superconducting quantum processors with control and readout electronics from Quantum Machines and cryogenic dilution refrigeration infrastructure from Bluefors. Rather than relying solely on cloud-based simulation or remote processor access, AAU will use the installation to train engineering students directly on cryogenic hardware operation, microwave control, and qubit readout, while supporting faculty research into component miniaturization, energy efficiency, and energy and materials modeling.
Based in Gothenburg, Sweden, and operating out of cleanroom facilities at Chalmers University of Technology, ConScience has grown organically without external venture capital by supplying superconducting qubit devices, including its Qubit-in-a-box product line, to academic laboratories and commercial quantum developers. Company executives noted that the Aalborg deployment marks an expansion from standalone quantum chips into integrated educational systems designed to address workforce bottlenecks in quantum hardware engineering.
