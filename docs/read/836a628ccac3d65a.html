IBM, RIKEN, and NVIDIA Achieve 79x Accuracy Gain in Quantum-Classical Simulations of Iron-Sulfur Clusters

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 20h quantum 453 words 3 min read

Researchers from IBM Quantum, RIKEN, and NVIDIA have demonstrated a 79-fold improvement in energy accuracy for quantum-classical simulations of strongly correlated iron-sulfur clusters by initializing Sample-Based Quantum Diagonalization (SQD) with broken-symmetry unrestricted Hartree-Fock (BS-UHF) reference states. Executed on the ibm_kingston quantum processor alongside RIKEN’s NVIDIA GB200-powered ROQUO supercomputer, the calculation brought ground-state energy estimates for a 72-qubit [Fe4S4(SCH3)4]2− active space within 4.3 millihartree of high-precision classical density-matrix renormalization group (DMRG) benchmarks.

Iron-sulfur cubane clusters play central roles in biological electron transfer and catalysis, but their antiferromagnetically coupled iron centers create dense multireference electronic correlations that challenge closed-shell quantum chemistry approximations. Previous SQD studies initialized from restricted Hartree-Fock (RHF) determinants stalled more than 500 millihartree above exact ground-state benchmarks even at large subspace dimensions.
By extending the open-source qcsc-prefect workflow to construct spin-polarized, fragment-localized Noodleman initial guesses verified via internal and external stability analysis, the team steered quantum sampling directly toward chemically relevant magnetic configurations. After validating the method on a 40-qubit [2Fe-2S] dimer using ibm_fez, the researchers scaled configuration recovery to 36 nodes (144 NVIDIA GB200 GPUs) of ROQUO and demonstrated cross-architecture checkpoint portability across JCAHPC’s Miyabi-G GPU cluster and RIKEN’s CPU-based Fugaku supercomputer.
Supported by Japan’s New Energy and Industrial Technology Development Organization (NEDO) under its quantum-supercomputer hybrid platform initiative, the study highlights reference-state preparation as a primary driver of SQD accuracy alongside subspace size and GPU-accelerated Davidson diagonalization.
