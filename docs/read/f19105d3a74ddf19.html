Intel to Acquire Classiq: Strategic Move Creates Full-Stack Quantum Computing Platform

Quantum Computing Report Doug Finke 2h quantum 805 words 5 min read

In a significant consolidation within the quantum computing sector, Intel has entered into a definitive agreement to acquire Israeli quantum software provider Classiq for approximately $2.3 billion. The acquisition is designed to combine Classiq’s automated algorithm synthesis platform with Intel’s scalable silicon spin-qubit hardware, officially positioning Intel as a full-stack quantum computing provider.
Bridging the Hardware-Software Gap The acquisition addresses a critical bottleneck in quantum development: the complexity of circuit design. Classiq’s synthesis platform abstracts this complexity—much like Electronic Design Automation (EDA) and Computer-Aided Design (CAD) revolutionized classical silicon chip design over forty years ago. By automating quantum algorithm synthesis, Classiq enables researchers and enterprise developers to build scalable quantum applications without needing deep expertise in the underlying hardware.
Intel has been developing its quantum capabilities since 2015, leveraging its advanced semiconductor manufacturing prowess to produce scalable silicon spin-qubit chips and cryogenic control electronics. By integrating Classiq’s high-level abstraction software, Intel aims to make its hardware far more accessible and user-friendly, pushing quantum technology closer to mainstream commercial viability.
An Open Ecosystem Approach Despite the acquisition by a major hardware manufacturer, Classiq’s platform will not become a closed system. Intel has committed to maintaining Classiq’s hardware- and modality-agnostic approach. Following the deal’s close, Intel will continue to support Classiq’s existing multi-platform integrations, ensuring an open and collaborative quantum software ecosystem.
Looking Ahead For Intel, the addition of Quantum Processing Units (QPUs) rounds out an already massive compute portfolio that includes CPUs, GPUs, and ASICs.
“Joining forces with Classiq transforms Intel’s Quantum Computing Group into a full-stack quantum computing business,” said Intel CEO Lip-Bu Tan, noting the complementary nature of Intel’s manufacturing scale and Classiq’s software innovation.
The transaction is subject to customary regulatory approvals and is expected to close in the first half of 2027. Until then, Intel and Classiq will continue to operate as independent entities.
Israel’s Quantum Frontier: How Targeted Capital and Deep-Tech Discipline Are Shaping a Global Hub
Israel has established itself as one of the world’s most concentrated and effective hubs for quantum technology. Despite its modest population size, Israel ranks #4 globally in private venture capital investment in the quantum sector, accounting for approximately 9% of total global private quantum VC funding (and roughly 15% when excluding the United States).
Over the past six years, the nation’s quantum startup count has expanded from five active companies in 2019 to over 20 commercial enterprises today, attracting between $650 million and $1 billion in total capital. This rapid scaling spans the entire technology stack, from trapped-ion and superconducting hardware to control electronics, software synthesis, and dual-use defense sensing.
A driving force behind Israel’s quantum investment engine is Entrée Capital, a leading venture firm managing $1.5 billion in assets under management. Operating under the guidance of Ran Achituv, General Partner at Entrée Capital and a key member of the Israel National Quantum Task Force, the firm has assembled a global portfolio of nine quantum investments spanning software, hardware, sensing, and communications. Achituv brings a background that bridges military intelligence, enterprise tech execution, and venture capital. Before entering venture capital, he founded the Israeli Defense Forces’ (IDF) Satellite Intelligence unit and served as Chief Technology Officer at Amdocs and senior executive at Verint.Investment Philosophy: Architectural Scalability Over Hype
Beyond software and trapped ions, Israel’s quantum landscape is defined by horizontal specialization and strong institutional backing:
- Superconducting & Cryogenic Scalability: Companies like QuamCore are addressing cryostat wiring bottlenecks by integrating digital superconducting control logic directly inside sub-Kelvin environments to enable 1-million-qubit scaling.
- Control Layer & Orchestration: Quantum Machines leads the global control hardware market with its Quantum Orchestration Platform (OPX), serving as a crucial classical-quantum interface across global research labs and commercial centers.
- Photonic & NV-Center Approaches: Quantum Source utilizes silicon photonics for room-temperature quantum computing systems, while NV-center pioneers leverage diamond-based spin hardware for edge-deployed processing.
- Dual-Use Sensing & Defense: Driven by the Directorate of Defense Research & Development (MAFAT) and firms like AccuBeat and Rafael, Israel is deploying quantum gravimeters, atomic clocks, and magnetometers for GPS-denied navigation, anti-spoofing defense, and secure communications.
While Israel excels at early-stage startup creation and seed-to-Series A funding, the ecosystem faces key growth hurdles:
- Late-Stage Capital Deficit: As hardware vendors transition toward multi-hundred-million-dollar capital expenditures for physical scale-up, startups rely heavily on international growth funds.
- Supply Chain Dependencies: Dependencies on specialized foreign suppliers for dilution refrigerators, high-power lasers, and microwave electronics introduce lead-time risks.
- Hybrid Enterprise Utility: The immediate focus for 2026–2029 centers on integrating QPUs into high-performance computing (HPC) centers and deploying hybrid quantum-classical workflows for enterprise adopters in defense, finance, and materials science.
Through the combination of institutional policy via the Israel National Quantum Initiative (INQI) and disciplined private capital deployment from investors like Entrée Capital, Israel continues to secure a critical position in the global quantum supply chain.
