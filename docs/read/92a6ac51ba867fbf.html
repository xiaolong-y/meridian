QUDORA Integrates NVIDIA CUDA-Q into Qamelion Emulator and Launches German-Funded QUANDA HPC Energy Consortium

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 19h quantum 352 words 2 min read

Braunschweig-based trapped-ion developer QUDORA Technologies has expanded its hybrid quantum-classical software stack through an integration with NVIDIA CUDA-Q alongside the launch of the three-year QUANDA (Quantum Anomaly Detection & Analysis) consortium. Together, the initiatives connect QUDORA’s Near Field Quantum Control (NFQC) microwave-addressed trapped-ion architecture with standard hybrid programming frameworks and supercomputing job pipelines ahead of physical processor deployment.
Through the CUDA-Q release, available via the qudora-sdk[cudaq] Python package and QUDORA Cloud, developers can execute hybrid quantum-classical workflows against Qamelion, QUDORA’s quantum emulator incorporating adaptive noise modeling. The environment enables researchers to benchmark measurement sensitivity and circuit behavior under realistic trapped-ion noise profiles before migrating identical CUDA-Q source code to physical hardware targets.
In parallel, QUDORA initiated the QUANDA project under the German Federal Ministry of Research, Technology and Space (BMFTR) Development of Digital Technologies program to automate trapped-ion access inside high-performance computing (HPC) centers. Working with GWDG, the joint computing center of the University of Göttingen and the Max Planck Society, alongside Leibniz University Hannover, Deggendorf Institute of Technology, UBT Future Energy Lab Wunsiedel, and ZukunftsEnergie Nordostbayern (ZENOB), the consortium will build an automated submission, transpilation, and execution pipeline evaluated across three energy-sector quantum machine learning anomaly detection workloads.
