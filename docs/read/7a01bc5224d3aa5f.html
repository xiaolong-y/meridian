Quantinuum Secures $1.5M in City and State LEDA Funding to Expand R&D Center in Albuquerque

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 12h quantum 325 words 2 min read

Trapped-ion quantum computing developer Quantinuum is expanding its operational presence in Albuquerque, New Mexico, supported by a joint $1.5 million USD state and municipal incentive package. The funding comprises $750,000 from the State of New Mexico and $750,000 from the City of Albuquerque via Local Economic Development Act (LEDA) funds to establish an expanded research and development hub focused on integrated photonics for trapped-ion processors.

Integrated Photonics Focus and Site Selection
The expanded facility will occupy a previously vacant site located at 5501 Wilshire in Albuquerque. Quantinuum will convert the property into advanced laboratory and office space dedicated to integrated photonics—a core hardware priority for its next-generation trapped-ion quantum architectures:
- Hardware Roadmap Alignment: The facility will focus on miniaturized, chip-scale photonics to guide, modulate, and deliver laser light for qubit state manipulation, addressability, and optical entanglement across multi-zone trapped-ion QPUs.
- National Laboratory Integration: The hub strengthens existing collaborative research ties with nearby federal research institutions, including Sandia National Laboratories, Los Alamos National Laboratory (LANL), and the University of New Mexico (UNM).
- Regional Technology Ecosystem: The expansion builds on New Mexico’s broader state initiative, which has directed over $450 million in capital toward quantum research, start-up infrastructure, and defense partnerships with organizations such as DARPA.
The Albuquerque R&D expansion complements Quantinuum’s corporate headquarters in Broomfield, Colorado, as well as its international technology centers across the U.S., UK, Germany, Japan, and Singapore.
