Canada Launches $20.3M Quantum Defence Innovation Secure Hub in Calgary

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 13h quantum 446 words 3 min read

Canadian Minister of National Defence David J. McGuinty has announced the launch of Canada’s first Quantum Defence Innovation Secure Hub (DISH) in Calgary, Alberta. Operated by a 13-member consortium led by the University of Calgary and its Quantum City initiative—under Managing Director Dr. Megan Lee—the hub will receive $20.3 million CAD over two years to accelerate the transition of domestic quantum research into mission-ready operational capabilities for the Canadian Armed Forces (CAF) and the Communications Security Establishment (CSE).
Established under the Department of National Defence’s BOREALIS (Bureau of Research, Engineering and Advanced Leadership in Innovation and Science) framework following a competitive Call for Proposals, the Quantum DISH provides a classified-ready, secure environment designed to overcome traditional procurement barriers. The consortium bridges Prairie-region post-secondary institutions—including the University of Calgary, University of Alberta, University of Lethbridge, and the University of Saskatchewan (supported by the Sylvia Fedoruk Canadian Centre for Nuclear Innovation and the quanTA center)—with premier defense and enterprise primes including Lockheed Martin Canada, General Dynamics Mission Systems–Canada, CAE, Dell Canada, and Calian.

Led by researchers such as Dr. Erika Janitz (Canada Research Chair in Quantum Hardware Engineering), the Quantum DISH focuses on four integrated technical work streams aimed at delivering validated prototypes within two years:
- Quantum Sensing & PNT: Developing satellite-independent Positioning, Navigation, and Timing (PNT) hardware capable of operating in Global Navigation Satellite System (GNSS)-denied environments, alongside electronic counter-countermeasures to detect and neutralize GPS spoofing.
- Quantum Communications & Cryptography: Establishing quantum-secure communications protocols, entanglement-based key distribution, and ultra-precise optical clock timing infrastructure for tactical networks.
- Quantum Algorithms & Decision Support: Translating mathematical optimization and quantum-hybrid machine learning into operational decision-support software for command-and-control logistics.
- Hardware Assurance: Validating the physical integrity, supply chain provenance, and tamper resistance of domestic and partner quantum devices prior to military deployment.
The Calgary hub joins existing BOREALIS initiatives—including the Maritime DISH and Uncrewed Systems DISH—as part of a broader national push to formalize sovereign capability priorities under Canada’s Defence Industrial Strategy. By embedding commercial defense primes directly into academic research centers like Quantum City, the program aims to shorten acquisition cycles and secure domestic supply chains for high-assurance quantum systems.
