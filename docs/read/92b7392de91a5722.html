Classiq Collaborations with Shionogi, KDDI Research, and SoftBank Earn Awards in Japan’s NEDO Quantum Challenge

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 13h quantum 446 words 3 min read

Quantum software developer Classiq announced that three joint enterprise research initiatives with Shionogi & Co., Ltd., KDDI Research, Inc., and SoftBank Corp. have received awards in Japan’s New Energy and Industrial Technology Development Organization (NEDO) Quantum Computing Challenge, organized under the Ministry of Economy, Trade and Industry (METI). Built using Classiq’s Qmod high-level modeling language and automated circuit synthesis engine, the projects evaluated near-term hardware execution alongside fault-tolerant resource requirements across pharmaceutical, cryptographic, and logistics workloads.

|---|---|---|
| Partner & Award | Application & Algorithmic Method | Measured Technical & Hardware Results |
| Shionogi & Co., Ltd. 1st Prize, Quality of Life | Benzene dimer π–π stacking via Deep QSCI divide-and-conquer active-space partitioning. | Cut register from 40 to 20 qubits (CAS(14e, 10o)); reached −0.91 kcal/mol at 4.0 Å vs. −1.14 kcal/mol CCSD(T). |
| KDDI Research (Q-Chainners) 3rd Prize, Society 5.0 | Quantum-walk cryptographic hash function generating fixed-length 256-bit digests. | ~49% simulated avalanche effect; validated block generation across 4 IBM processors. |
| SoftBank Corp. Outstanding Research Award | Hybrid QML area partitioning for 1,921 Tokyo delivery nodes plus FTQC search routing. | 2.31% shorter drone delivery distance on quantum hardware vs. classical baseline. |
In the Shionogi collaboration, detailed in an accompanying preprint, researchers combined Deep VQE subsystem partitioning with quantum-selected configuration interaction (Deep QSCI) to model noncovalent aromatic stacking without repeating quantum sampling across intermolecular separations. By reusing a single 20-qubit monomer QSCI calculation to construct an 8,649-dimensional effective dimer Hamiltonian under the 6-31G** basis set, the workflow avoided direct 40-qubit dimer circuits while identifying orbital consistency and charge-transfer extensions as key requirements for broader basis-set convergence. In computational chemistry, a basis set defines the mathematical functions used to approximate electron orbitals around atoms. The two asterisks in 6-31G** denote added polarization functions on both carbon and hydrogen atoms, giving simulated electron clouds the directional flexibility needed to capture weak intermolecular attractions.
Meanwhile, KDDI Research integrated its quantum-walk digests into a Proof-of-Quantum-Work (PoQ) blockchain prototype, and SoftBank paired near-term hardware partitioning with gate-level resource estimates for fault-tolerant quantum search routing.
Separately, Classiq entered a strategic partnership with Munich-based digital transformation consultancy Ventum Consulting to expand enterprise quantum readiness across Germany. Under the agreement, the two firms will work jointly with German small and medium-sized enterprises and large corporations to evaluate business use cases, build internal technical expertise, and prototype hardware-portable quantum applications on the Classiq platform.
