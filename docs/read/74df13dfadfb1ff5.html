Quandela Establishes South Korea as Asian Hub for Photonic QPU Manufacturing and Hardware R&D

Quantum Computing Report Mohamed Abdel-Kareem 1d quantum 375 words 2 min read

French photonic quantum computing manufacturer Quandela has unveiled a strategic framework establishing South Korea as its primary Asian hub for quantum processor manufacturing, system integration, and collaborative R&D. Announced in Seoul, the initiative targets semiconductor-level fabrication of integrated photonic QPU modules, component co-development, and hybrid high-performance computing (HPC) software integration, complementing the company’s regional operations across Europe and North America (via its Quandela Federal defense subsidiary).
The manufacturing strategy seeks to form an industrial consortium involving South Korean semiconductor foundries (such as Samsung Electronics and SK hynix) and optoelectronic system integrators (including LG Innotek) to scale mass production of photonic control chips and optical component packaging. Under the proposed intellectual property framework, pre-existing IP remains with individual partners, while co-developed manufacturing protocols and integrated optical modules will be shared based on institutional contributions.

The initiative integrates with ongoing QPU software optimizations. Collaborating with NVIDIA, Quandela reduced GPU-to-QPU communication latency to the millisecond regime, enabling high-speed hybrid error mitigation and job execution across HPC supercomputing clusters (such as the 12-qubit Lucy processor deployed at France’s TGCC facility). Simultaneously, hardware engineering teams from KAIST are deploying to Paris to integrate Korean-fabricated electronic control units into Quandela’s physical QPU chassis, establishing a cross-border supply chain for full-stack fault-tolerant quantum computing (FTQC).
